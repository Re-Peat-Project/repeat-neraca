# Water sharing / allocation and canal-operation decision support for tropical peatlands (KHG), Indonesia focus

Method note: ~17 tool calls (web search; several full-text fetches failed: P.10/2019 PDF too large, ppkl.menlhk.go.id unresolvable, hazeportal redirect). Many findings below come from search-result summaries rather than full primary text; items I could not confirm are in Gaps. Research date context: 2026-10. Regulation vs practice is labelled explicitly.

## 1. How do existing frameworks allocate/share water between restoration, plantations, smallholders, fire prevention and ecosystem needs?

### Takeaway
Indonesian peat law does not allocate water volumes between users. It zones land by function (lindung/protected vs budidaya/cultivation) per KHG and imposes a water-table compliance rule (0.4 m) at designated compliance points; "sharing" is implicit via function zoning and per-concession obligations. Tidal-swamp agriculture (PUPR) has a separate, explicit operation-plan regime (gate operating procedures), which is the closest existing formal analogue to water-sharing rules.

### Cited Findings
Regulation
- P.16/MENLHK/SETJEN/KUM.1/2/2017 is the technical guideline for peat ecosystem function restoration (Berita Negara 2017 No. 338); a Director General regulation P.3/2018 governs construction of rewetting infrastructure (sekat kanal etc.) under it — [peraturan.go.id BN 338/2017](https://peraturan.go.id/files/bn338-2017.pdf); [pasal.id listing](https://pasal.id/peraturan/permen/permen-lhk-no-p-16-menlhk-setjen-kum-1-2-2017-tahun-2017); [P.3/2018 PDF (could not be fetched)](https://ppkl.menlhk.go.id/website/filebox/790/191001094842PERDIRJEN%20PPKL%20Nomor%20P%203_2018%20-%20Pembangunan%20Infrastruktur%20Pembasahan.PDF)
- Permen LHK P.10/2019 covers peat management by KHG (full text not read; available at [UGM copy](https://luk.staff.ugm.ac.id/atur/sda/PermenLHK10-2019PengelolaanGambutKHG.pdf)). KHG framework is also referenced to P.14/2017 in a Riau study — [Riau KHG protection policy](https://jurnal.usk.ac.id/kanun/article/view/35392)
- PP 57/2016 replaced/amended PP 71/2014 (signed 2 Dec 2016). Peat is deemed damaged if groundwater is 0.4 m or deeper below the peat surface or pyritic sediment is exposed. Proposals for 0.6 m from business, the Agriculture Ministry and some experts were rejected; 0.4 m retained — [Mongabay 2016 (legal notes)](https://mongabay.co.id/2016/12/15/catatan-hukum-peraturan-gambut-lama-vs-baru/); [Mongabay 2016 (changes)](https://mongabay.co.id/2016/12/05/presiden-tandatangani-revisi-aturan-gambut-berikut-poin-poin-perubahan/amp/)
- Measurement is at compliance points (titik penaatan); per the minister, at least 15% of main-crop plots/production blocks are designated compliance points. Cultivation function can be converted to protected function if criteria are met — [Mongabay 2016 (legal notes)](https://mongabay.co.id/2016/12/15/catatan-hukum-peraturan-gambut-lama-vs-baru/)
- Zoning: in Riau, 59 KHG cover ~5.0 Mha, 44.29% protected function and 55.71% cultivation function — [Riau KHG study](https://jurnal.usk.ac.id/kanun/article/view/35392)
- Critics: HKTI said 0.4 m at all times is impractical for smallholder oil palm; academics argued water table alone is an inadequate damage criterion — [Hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/berita/a/hkti-nilai-pp-gambut-rugikan-kebun-sawit-rakyat-lt5888917dcd26e/); [Kontan](https://industri.kontan.co.id/news/kebijakan-perlindungan-gambut-ditentang-akademisi)
- Tidal swamp (rawa pasang surut): Permen PUPR 11/2015 regulates operation and maintenance (eksploitasi dan pemeliharaan) of tidal swamp reclamation networks; its annex says the operation plan comprises a cropping plan and a water-management plan, translated into operating procedures for water-control gates. Permen PUPR 16/2015 is the equivalent for lebak swamp and specifies seasonal, weekly and daily operation plans — [PermenPUPR 11/2015](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/152412/PermenPUPR11-2015.pdf); [Annex 1](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/152413/PermenPUPR11-2015%20-%20Lamp.1.pdf); [PermenPUPR 16/2015](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/152425/PermenPUPR16-2015.pdf)
- Tidal flooding classes A-D (A: flooded at all tides; B: only spring tides; C: not flooded; D: groundwater >50 cm, tide felt in tertiary canals) are literature classifications used to set water management and cropping, not text of the Permen — [Unsri source via search](https://conference.unsri.ac.id/index.php/semnashas/article/download/2101/1220)

Practice
- BRG/BRGM 3R: rewetting (canal blocking, canal backfilling, deep wells), revegetation, revitalisation of livelihoods — [Sutikno et al. 2019](https://luk.tsipil.ugm.ac.id/rawa/2019SigitSutikno-CanalBlockingRiauTropicalPeatland.pdf)
- A recent review of tidal swamp irrigation operation (2026) states groundwater and salinity respond with different lags per topography/soil, requiring adaptive daily/seasonal gate/pump operation; in acidic peat, one-way (flap gate) operation is often needed — [ASTONJADRO](https://ejournal.uika-bogor.ac.id/index.php/ASTONJADRO/article/download/22744/9002/120537) (secondary, from search summary)
- Telang I (South Sumatra) work stresses farmers' ability to control the tertiary-block water table and links it to network control structures; DRAINMOD/Outlaw-type simulation used for design — [Imanudin et al. 2011](https://repository.unsri.ac.id/23948/)
- Paludiculture: Wetlands International/UN-REDD claim viability vs oil palm (sago with 350 households over 250 ha; purun when water tables are high); these are project-sponsor claims; value chains and market recognition are bottlenecks; socioeconomic evidence thin — [UN-REDD](https://un-redd.org/news/using-local-food-crops-promote-sustainable-food-systems-and-preserve-peatlands); [Wetlands Int'l paludiculture](https://peatlands.wetlands.org/paludiculture-in-indonesia/); [IOP sago value chain](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1755-1315/914/1/012032)

### Inferences
- No instrument found sets a volumetric or priority-based water-sharing rule across users in a KHG; the effective "allocation" is a head (water level) target per function zone. A water-sharing analysis would therefore have to define demand as a water-level/storage target per land-use class, not a flow demand (my inference).
- The PUPR operation-plan structure (seasonal/weekly/daily plans, gate procedures) is the best institutional template to borrow for peat canal/gate rules.
- Interacting issue: 0.4 m cap vs plantation drainability (flooding risk to crops in wet season) is the core allocation conflict, visible in the 0.6 m debate.

### Gaps
- Full text of P.10/2019, P.16/2017 and P.3/2018 not read: article numbers, exact compliance-point rules, and whether the 0.4 m is a daily/monthly/annual criterion are unverified here.
- Permen PUPR 11/2015 gate rules by flooding class not extracted.
- No source found on concession-vs-smallholder canal conflicts or formal inter-user water agreements at KHG level; no Sugihan-specific gate schedules found.
- Whether PP 57/2016 / P.16/2017 remain unchanged as of 2026 (e.g., Omnibus/Cipta Kerja amendments) not checked.

## 2. Existing operational systems and decision-support tools

### Takeaway
Indonesia has monitoring platforms (PRIMS, SIPALAGA, SiPongi, GFW) and a research base for canal-block hydrological modelling and optimisation, but I found no operational tool that combines canal-network hydraulics, multi-user demands and forecasts into gate/block operating decisions. Fire-danger indices that include groundwater are research products.

### Cited Findings
- PRIMS (Peatland Restoration Information Management System): web spatial platform for restoration progress; can record water level and soil moisture via satellite imagery; priority provinces Central/South/West Kalimantan, Jambi, Riau, South Sumatra. SIPALAGA described as a fire early-detection system (per BRG head, 2019), and associated with ~150 groundwater-level devices in seven priority provinces — [Jakarta Post 2019](https://newgelora.thejakartapost.com/news/2019/06/21/indonesian-peatland-agency-develops-online-platform-to-monitor-restoration); [CIFOR slides](https://www.slideshare.net/CIFOR/monitoring-peatland-restoration-in-indonesia)
- BRGM installs groundwater level monitoring infrastructure (GWLMI) and has a fire danger system said to forecast drought/fire vulnerability 7 days ahead (journalistic source) — [Jakarta Post 2023](https://www.thejakartapost.com/adv/2023/12/20/ensuring-safe-peatland.html)
- Canal-block optimisation: Urzainki et al. 2020 couple a Boussinesq-type hydrological model with genetic algorithm and simulated annealing to place limited blocks in a 931 km2 drained Sumatran peatland; optimised layouts beat traditional placement at equal cost (single-objective: water table at fixed cost) — [Biogeosciences 17, 4769](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/17/4769/2020/bg-17-4769-2020.html)
- CIFOR-ICRAF meso-scale canal-blocking hydrological model at KHG scale, intended for restoration planning, can simulate plantation scenarios — [CIFOR brief](https://cifor-icraf.org/publications/pdf/project-briefs/Brief-Canal-Blocking.pdf)
- Process-based modelling of 168 canal blocks over ~22,000 ha in Sumatra: net positive water-table effect but lowering in some areas; monitoring data sparse and clustered around canals — [Biogeosciences preprint bg-2022-218](https://bg.copernicus.org/preprints/bg-2022-218/bg-2022-218.pdf)
- HEC-RAS used for site-level canal block studies (Merbau, Riau; thesis-level, redesign to 7 m base, 2.5 m depth; 48% simulated effectiveness at minimum discharge) — [UniMed abstract](https://digilib.unimed.ac.id/67499/3/5213250047_Abstrak.pdf); Freewat model for Sebangau: 0.6 m block height satisfactory at all spacings tested, using -0.4 m as benchmark — [UNRI](https://jom.unri.ac.id/index.php/JOMFTEKNIK/article/download/21323/20632)
- Fire indices with groundwater: Peat Fire Vulnerability Index (Taufik et al. 2022): high danger mostly when water table below 40 cm; burned area only below 60 cm; observed fires fell in high/extreme classes; modified KBDI (South Sumatra) with modelled groundwater: classes <100, 101-150, >150, hotspots in the high class; Hayasaka et al. 2020: GWL thresholds for three fire stages between -300 and -500 mm — [ASEAN Haze Portal (Taufik)](https://hazeportal.asean.org/?p=12604); [SWAT-MODFLOW index](https://hazeportal.asean.org/?p=12617); [Indonesia Water Portal on Hayasaka](https://indonesiawaterportal.com/news/peatland-fires-in-indonesia-can-be-predicted-by-new-evaluation-approach-study-finds/)
- Seasonal forecasting: ECMWF SEAS5 evaluated for Riau peat water table/fire (2015 drought); ProbFire using SEAS5 outperformed climatology at 3-5 month lead times in S. Kalimantan, S. Sumatra, S. Papua (Central Sumatra only at shorter leads) — [ESSOAr](https://essopenarchive.org/doi/full/10.1002/essoar.10501280.1); [NHESS preprint](https://nhess.copernicus.org/preprints/nhess-2021-245/nhess-2021-245.pdf)
- Malaysia: RSPO smallholder BMP target of 60 cm (50-70) below drain bank or 50 cm (40-60) in monitoring wells; one stop-off per ~20 cm water-level difference along collection drains; low-cost wood/sandbag weirs; MPOB DG cites 40-60 cm under MSPO/RSPO for fire control (the formal MPOB guideline not found) — [RSPO manual](https://rspo.org/library/lib_files/preview/954); [RSPO presentation](https://rt10.rspo.org/ckfinder/userfiles/files/PC5_5%20Faizal%20Parish%20Presentation.pdf); [Borneo Post](https://www.pressreader.com/malaysia/the-borneo-post/20221118/282256669488341)
- PeatWaterWise (Cambridge) is a water-table decision-support tool for peat agriculture (Europe-oriented) — [Cambridge](https://www.enterprise.cam.ac.uk/?p=37966)
- Large restoration trial (Sumatra): 257 compacted peat dams raised water levels over 4800 ha; water tables from ~-0.6 to ~-0.3 m halved subsidence — [PMC11087581](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11087581/)

### Inferences
- Optimisation literature optimises block placement against water-table benefit and cost; a water-sharing analysis adds competing user demands, which I did not find formulated as multi-objective in the sources retrieved.
- The data constraint (sparse, canal-clustered wells) limits calibration/validation of any KHG-scale model.

### Gaps
- Not verified: SiPongi's methodology (FWI/DC use, BMKG inputs), BMKG operational seasonal products, GFW alerts specifics, Deltares/Wetlands Int'l named tools, SOBEK/Delft3D peat applications, current status (2026) of SIPALAGA/PRIMS, and any multi-objective (as opposed to single-objective) canal-block studies. The "SESAME" name had no hits.
- No source found on water-use-efficiency indicators or relative water deficit applied to peat KHG.

## 3. What "operational" means (latency, automation, horizons, stakeholders, outputs, governance, metrics)

### Takeaway
Evidence retrieved only partially defines this: existing systems are ~weekly-to-7-day fire-danger products and monitoring dashboards, with seasonal (3-5 month) probabilistic skill from SEAS5; gate-operation practice uses seasonal/weekly/daily plans in PUPR swamp schemes. A formal operational definition for peat water sharing must be assembled from these parts.

### Cited Findings
- PUPR swamp O&M regime uses seasonal, weekly and daily operation plans for gates — [PermenPUPR 16/2015](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/152425/PermenPUPR16-2015.pdf)
- BRGM fire danger forecast lead 7 days (journalistic) — [Jakarta Post 2023](https://www.thejakartapost.com/adv/2023/12/20/ensuring-safe-peatland.html)
- SEAS5-based skill at 3-5 month leads for fire probability in southern regions — [NHESS preprint](https://nhess.copernicus.org/preprints/nhess-2021-245/nhess-2021-245.pdf)
- Compliance monitoring regime: dipwells at designated compliance points (>=15% of blocks) — [Mongabay](https://mongabay.co.id/2016/12/15/catatan-hukum-peraturan-gambut-lama-vs-baru/)
- Stakeholders seen in sources: KLHK, BRGM, PUPR, Pemda/KHG managers, concessionaires, villages (BRG priority villages), RSPO/MSPO auditors. Water-level management should avoid wet-season flooding from blocks — [Merbau Riau study (via search)](https://digilib.unimed.ac.id/67499/3/5213250047_Abstrak.pdf)

### Inferences
- Three nested horizons are natural: daily/weekly gate rules (tidal/rainfall), 1-4 week fire-danger lead, seasonal (3-5 month) deficit outlook for dry-season planning.
- Latency of well data (manual dipwells vs telemetry) is the limiting factor; I found no published latency figures.

### Gaps
- No documented governance arrangement (who may operate shared blocks, authority across concessions/villages) or performance metrics for operational peat water systems. Data latency of PRIMS/SIPALAGA/SiPongi not found.

## 4. Indicators/outputs a water-sharing analysis should report

### Takeaway
Sources support a few threshold-based indicators directly (days/time with water table deeper than 0.4 m, 0.6 m fire-onset threshold, block-induced rise and radius); the rest (storage deficit, fraction of demand met, equity) are design proposals without a retrieved Indonesian precedent.

### Cited Findings
- Regulatory threshold: 0.4 m below peat surface (damage criterion) at compliance points — [Mongabay](https://mongabay.co.id/2016/12/15/catatan-hukum-peraturan-gambut-lama-vs-baru/)
- Fire thresholds: high danger below ~40 cm, burned area below ~60 cm (Taufik et al.); fire-stage GWL -300 to -500 mm (Hayasaka) — [ASEAN Haze Portal](https://hazeportal.asean.org/?p=12604); [Indonesia Water Portal](https://indonesiawaterportal.com/news/peatland-fires-in-indonesia-can-be-predicted-by-new-evaluation-approach-study-finds/)
- Effectiveness metrics in field studies: GWL rise (10-21 cm), wetting distance (>75 m; ~170 m at Sungai Tohor) — [Dompas study via UNRI](https://jom.unri.ac.id/index.php/JOMFTEKNIK/article/download/26332/25476); [Sutikno et al. 2019](https://luk.tsipil.ugm.ac.id/rawa/2019SigitSutikno-CanalBlockingRiauTropicalPeatland.pdf)
- Subsidence rate as outcome (halved at -0.3 vs -0.6 m) — [PMC11087581](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11087581/)
- Oil palm target ranges for agronomy (Malaysia): 40-60 / 50-70 cm — [RSPO manual](https://rspo.org/library/lib_files/preview/954)
- Climatic controls: KHG analysis identified February and November as months controlling groundwater-based management success (Pulang Pisau/Central Kalimantan type analysis) — [IPB spatial modelling](https://jai.ipb.ac.id/index.php/jpsl/article/view/38673)

### Inferences (proposed, not sourced)
- Per land-use/zone (lindung, HTI, sawit, smallholder, paludiculture): fraction of days with water table shallower than 0.4 m (regulatory), shallower than 0.6 m (fire), and above a flood-damage ceiling (crop-specific); storage deficit relative to target-level storage; canal level/gate-rule recommendations per segment; fraction of "demand" (target-level storage) met by user class; equity as spread (e.g., Gini or min/max ratio) of compliance across users/zones; uncertainty bands given sparse data. A fire-season "days below threshold" outlook can use SEAS5-driven scenarios.
- Because regulation is a single water-table threshold, reporting both the regulatory and fire/agronomic thresholds avoids implying the law defines allocation.

### Gaps
- No Indonesian precedent for equity/fraction-of-demand-met metrics, and no retrieved source on water-use-efficiency indicators in peat settings.
