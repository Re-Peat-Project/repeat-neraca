## 1. Re-peat scope, architecture, components, languages, docs, goals, study areas, stakeholders, deliverables

### Takeaway
Re-peat is the hub (docs, data contracts, per-site configs and runbooks) of a World Bank/KLH-funded peatland rewetting toolkit run by the GIS workstream at UGM Geodesy; it contains no pipeline code and delegates to seven Python module repos that exchange files only. The stated end product is an interactive water-sharing decision dashboard for KLH/BPLH and the new national peat agency (BPGMN), but as of 2026-10-20 only terrain, delineation, prioritisation, a pump/route QGIS module, SiTMAT download, and a KHG-level neraca deficit exist; the water-sharing hydrology model, canal-network module, allocation, scenarios and dashboard are specified but not coded.

**Stated goals and decision questions**
- Problem framing: "Indonesia's peat fire problem is a water problem"; regulation (PP 71/2014 as amended by PP 57/2016) treats cultivation-function peat as damaged if TMA is >0.4 m below surface at a compliance point; toolkit answers (1) where to focus (which KHG/sub-KHG), (2) what ground surface to use (DEMNAS is a DSM, accuracy metres), (3) how to get water there (canals, blocks, pumps) — [D-README] lines 5-18.
- Methodology (dated "Oct 3, 2026", author Dany Laksono): model "allocates scarce dry-season water inside a KHG — held by canal blocks, routed by water gates, and imported by pumps — so that the water table stays no deeper than 0.4 m below the peat surface where fire risk and burn history are highest. It runs as an interactive dashboard for KLH/BPLH and the new national peat agency (BPGMN)" — [METH] line 7. Four decision questions: where next N blocks; which compartments existing blocks/gates can keep wet vs need pumps; where pumps draw/discharge, capacity, hours, fuel budget; which areas stay above 0.4 m after all interventions and need fire-suppression readiness — [METH] lines 13-18.
- Scope: one KHG at a time, daily time step, dry-season focus; out of scope: ignition behaviour, fire spread, firefighting tactics, carbon accounting — [METH] line 20.
- Context cited by the methodology: only ~45,430 of an ideal 538,568 canal blocks exist across seven fire-prone provinces (KLH/BPLH, 18 Jun 2026); KLH/BPLH "already applies real-time groundwater monitoring and water sharing in the dry season"; pump relays of 11 km (Jambi) and 9 km (Ketapang) are field practice — [METH] lines 9-11 (sources listed there; several marked as seen only as search summaries in the References, [METH] line 595).
- Institutional context: BRG/BRGM mandate ended 31 Dec 2024, who hosts SiPALAGA/PRIMS data now is "not publicly documented"; BPGMN created by presidential decision 24 Sep 2026, Perpres in draft early Oct, "its prioritisation method is not yet defined, which is an opening"; "No published method ranks areas inside a KHG by hydrology, feasibility and cost. That is the gap this toolkit fills" — [D01] lines 30-36.
- Ancestor study: 2021 Kahayan-Sebangau final report (16 sub-KHG, metrik IPG, water balance dS = R(1-C) - ET); documented errors to avoid (treating water-table decline as water volume, i.e. Sy = 1; "108 days" claim; canal emptying 730-770 d) — [D01] lines 51-72; [D06] lines 6-8.

### Inferences
- The hub's status table (written pre-split) is partly out of date: `repeat-pump` (routing, sekat siting, pump sizing, QGIS plugin, a Stage-12 2D groundwater block optimiser ported from the same Urzainki solver as repeat-neraca's testbed) now exists, so "Route and block siting not yet coded" is no longer accurate. The canal-network "keystone" is partly delivered inside `repeat-pump` (`topology.py`, `network.py`, `segments.py`) but not as the shared module the build plan envisages.
- Operational = not yet a Re-peat objective in the delivered code: everything run so far is a retrospective/test run over a 2026 window on two KHG; the "dashboard" exists as static HTML reports (prioritisation, neraca process pages) not as a live service.
- Water sharing, in the project's own terminology, is an intra-KHG allocation of canal/gate/pump water, not a basin-scale river-water allocation. Any gap analysis for "water sharing" must decide which meaning it uses.

### Gaps
- No ADRs, no issue tracker export, no CI, no release/versioning policy, no deployment/hosting documentation anywhere in the hub; the only "operational" language is the dashboard description in [METH] and `docs/04` "Operations calendar" (lines near the end of [D04]).
- No explicit project timeline, milestone dates or funder deliverable list was found in the repos (the milestone list M0-M6 in [D08] lines 122-134 is technical, undated).
- Stakeholder agreements (KLH data-sharing, SiPALAGA hosting, BPGMN data mandate) are listed as unknown, not documented ([D05] line 169; [METH] lines 422, 433).
- The two PDFs in `docs/` (Padang RGP topo-hydro study, KHG SK SS final report) were not read (binary); their content is unknown to this audit.

## 2. Data, pipelines, satellite products, in-situ data and models a water-balance model could consume

### Takeaway
Re-peat already ingests, through `repeat-common` (STAC/COG/Zarr, not GEE), ERA5 hourly rain and evaporation, Sentinel-2 NDMI, MODIS burned area, TerraClimate, WorldCover, JRC water, FIRMS, CHIRPS and WaPOR (experimental), SiTMAT water tables (2026 only), project-supplied DTM, peat depth, canals and blocks (REPEAT-LH files), and a Sentinel-1 backscatter experiment (in repeat-neraca). Not present or not wired in: IMERG/GSMaP/CHIRPS at operational reliability, MODIS/other ET beyond WaPOR tests, soil-moisture products, InSAR subsidence, river stage/discharge, tide, reservoir/pump inventories, concession water-level targets, multi-year water-table records, and any operational scheduler.

### Cited Findings

**Where data live and how they are ingested**
- Raw/derived data are never in git: `REPEAT_DATA` (REPEAT-LH `_data`: `Peat depth and DTM/...`, `_converted/...`), `REPEAT_WORK` (clipped vectors, caches, outputs; "today `khg-prioritisation-module/data_local`"), `SITMAT_PROCESSED` — [README] lines 25-37. Site configs expand `${VAR}` and "a missing one stops the run" — [D11] line 96; `GH\repeat-neraca\src\repeat_neraca\inputs.py` lines 23-35.
- Ingest mechanism is Python cloud-native readers (STAC + odc.stac, Zarr, COG, direct HTTP), not GEE: Planetary Computer STAC `https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/api/stac/v1` with signed assets; collections `sentinel-2-l2a`, `modis-64A1-061`, `terraclimate`, `esa-worldcover`, `jrc-gsw`; ERA5 from Google ARCO Zarr `gcp-public-data-arco-era5 ... full_37-1h-0p25deg-chunk-1.zarr-v3`; CHIRPS COGs from `data.chc.ucsb.edu`; FIRMS VIIRS country CSVs — `GH\repeat-common\src\repeat_common\sources.py` lines 39-64; [D-README... prioritas_khg README] line 12 ("the Python alternative to a GEE workflow ... STAC + COG/Zarr"). GEE appears only as optional/legacy in `repeat-pump` (`ee_fetch.py`) and as "not tested" provider in `GH\repeat-prioritisation\docs\data_sources.md` (Providers table).
- Cache is per KHG on a UTM grid, "keyed by file name, not by the settings that made a layer" (stale-cache hazard) — [KG]\README.md lines 24-25.

**Satellite / reanalysis products already read (and their tested state)**
- Live test 2026-10-15 (`GH\repeat-prioritisation\docs\data_sources.md`, "Live fetch test"): Sentinel-2 NDMI works; MODIS burned area works; TerraClimate works after fixes; WorldCover, JRC surface water work; FIRMS works; CHIRPS dry-spell fetch failed with HTTP 403 (burst-triggered block, later HEAD returned 200 on 2026-10-16, reads not retried). Decision 2026-10-15: ERA5 hourly precipitation replaces CHIRPS as the rainfall source.
- ERA5: 0.25 deg (~25 km), hourly, Google ARCO Zarr; each hour ~0.3 s whatever the window; one KHG-year of dry months ~4 min with 8 readers, a full year ~13,000 hourly reads (~18-22 min); Google answered 503 to 16 parallel readers — same file, "Rainfall: ERA5 replaces CHIRPS" section. Also gives evaporation and ERA5-based fallback for TerraClimate gap (2022+).
- Dataset end dates that bind an operational system: TerraClimate ends 2021-12; `jrc-gsw` ends 2020; `gpm-imerg-hhr` ends 2021-05 on Planetary Computer; WorldCover only 2020 and 2021; Planetary Computer ERA5 (`era5-pds`) only to 2020-12 and has no evaporation; Copernicus CLMS evapotranspiration 300 m only from 2025-11 — same file, "Open problems" and "Providers".
- Candidate-but-untested list (research 2026-10-16): CHIRPS v2/v3 5 km daily, GSMaP 0.1 deg (JAXA registration), IMERG 0.1 deg (Earthdata login), ERA5-Land 0.1 deg (CDS login), MODIS MOD16A2GF 500 m 8-day, WaPOR v3 level 1 300 m 10-day ("documented for Africa and the Near East, check Indonesian quality"), SMAP L4 9 km, CLMS soil water index 12.5 km, Sentinel-1 RTC 10-20 m — same file, "Finer rainfall, evaporation and water-management data" table.
- WaPOR and CHIRPS daily readers exist in code (`repeat_common.forcing.wapor_daily`, `chirps_daily`, lines 144-195 of `GH\repeat-common\src\repeat_common\forcing.py`) and WaPOR is used as the alternative/bound evaporation in the KHG deficit (`E_wapor_mm`, `e_wapor_covers_period`) — [D11] line 62-67, 75; `GH\repeat-neraca\src\repeat_neraca\deficit.py` lines 25-30, 67-79. CHIRPS is in `forcing_for` as a "finer test" only; the exported `forcing_daily.csv` has no CHIRPS/IMERG column ([D11] line 66).
- BMKG: open-data portal gives 3-day village forecasts only (no observations/HTH); HTH is published as maps/images only; BMKG GIS ArcGIS server hosts Fire Danger Rating System layers (Drought Code with day 0-7 forecast, DMC, FFMC, BUI, ISI, FWI; ~0.008-0.02 deg), latest-value snapshots with no history (needs daily archiving); Data Online requires account + CAPTCHA — `data_sources.md` "BMKG" section. None is ingested; a `bmkg_catalogue.py` probe script exists in `GH\repeat-prioritisation\scripts\`.
- Sentinel-1 for water-table proxy: experiment `repeat_neraca.bucket.sentinel1` (Planetary Computer RTC). Within-station brighter VV scene coincides with shallower water table (median Spearman -0.38, 84% of stations negative, Kampar-Gaung; -0.62, 98%, Sugihan-Lumpur); across stations VV-VH ratio ranks peak depth in Kampar-Gaung (0.53; out-of-fold RMSE 20.6 vs 24.6 cm no-skill) but concession explains 64% of variance and "radar sees management differences between concessions more than differences inside them"; no across-station signal in Sugihan-Lumpur (<=0.17) — `data_sources.md` last section; [SL]\README.md lines 70-72; [KG]\README.md (neraca section). The methodology treats Sentinel-1/InSAR/OPTRAM only as checks, not inputs, until compared against loggers ([METH] lines 562-564, 552-560).
- InSAR subsidence, ICESat-2/GEDI (via SlideRule) exist as: ICESat-2/GEDI used for DEM calibration (repeat-dem); InSAR "strongest candidate for the next addition" but not built ([METH] line 550).

**Static layers**
- DTM: project REPEAT-LH DTM rasters (EPSG:4326, e.g. 13,794 x 7,232 px, 0.000111111 deg = ~12 m) per KHG (`DTM_Sumatera-002/...`); valid on only ~47% of the Kampar-Gaung sample grid and ~54% for Sugihan-Lumpur; percentiles show floor/wide range — [KG]\reports\kampar_gaung_inputs.md lines 14-19; [SL]\reports\sugihan_lumpur_inputs.md lines 14-19. - Peat hydraulic parameters: Urzainki et al. (2020/2023) tables; the 2020 table is boreal (Paivanen retention) per `NOTICE-urzainki2020.md`; tropical sets (Urzainki 2023 small/large) are options, "site-calibrated, so adopting one needs a decision" — [D12] line 16; `GH\repeat-neraca\docs\tropical-peat-hydraulic-parameters.md`.

## 4. Re-peat plans for water management, water sharing, restoration scenarios, dashboards, operational deployment that neraca must support

### Takeaway
The plan (methodology + build plan) is a layered decision model: Tier 1 compartment hydrology (daily) feeding a weekly rolling-horizon min-cost-flow/LP allocation of gate and pump water, a strategy tree, S0-S5 scenario packages across normal/moderate/strong El Nino years, Monte Carlo with terrain error, and an interactive dashboard with offline-cached uncertainty. Neraca's role in this plan is Tier 1/Tier 2 hydrology (the `repeat_neraca` package was literally the `watershare` M0 package) and the KHG deficit; the allocation, strategy, scenarios, calibration, Monte Carlo and dashboard stages are all unbuilt. Operational deployment (scheduled runs, forecasts, hosting) is mentioned only as "operational use needs a rainfall forecast or ensemble".

### Cited Findings

**Water management / water sharing model**
- Tier 1: daily compartment water balance `S_y(zeta) A dh/dt = A(P - ET) - C (h - h_c) + Q_pump` with exponential `S_y(zeta) = s1 exp(s2 zeta)` (s1 0.3, ~0.1 at 1.5 m), canal exchange conductance `C = K d_eff (2 L_reach)/L_inf`, ET baseline 4.17 mm/day scaled by land cover and rooting-depth stress; canal reaches as storage cells, hourly sub-stepping; within-compartment exponential profile to report fraction of area meeting the 0.4 m target — [METH] lines 143-160. Tier 2: 2D Boussinesq (Urzainki 2023) offline for pilot KHG to calibrate Tier 1 — [METH] lines 162-168.
- Interventions: blocks (weir + leakage, Villemonte submergence), gates (Hold/Share/Spill rules, season switch date), pumps (`Q_p = min(Q_max, Q_src) H_op/24`, fuel budget, relay chains with seepage credited as distributed rewetting, flag pumps without holding structure) — [METH] lines 170-206.
- Allocation: weekly decision interval, deficit per compartment from banded water-table profile, priority weights (risk x burn history x protected 1.5 x village), supply = internal surplus + gravity transfer + pumped supply, min-cost network flow/LP with fuel penalty, rolling horizon H=3 intervals (forecast/ensemble in operational use), retention weight, greedy fallback; planning-mode greedy block siting with marginal benefit curve — [METH] lines 208-254.
- Strategy tree: 4 tests in cost order (meets target >=95% season days; gravity supply; block in reach; pump reach), fallback "fire readiness" — [METH] lines 280-297. Fire risk module: hazard from modelled depth classes 0.4/0.6/0.85 m and dry spells x susceptibility terciles — [METH] lines 256-278 (PFVI deferred).
- Scenarios S0 baseline, S1 repair, S2 proposed blocks, S3 optimised blocks, S4 gate operation + gravity sharing, S5 pumps/relays within fuel budget, crossed with climate years (analogs 2015 and 2019, or any year with forcing incl. 2023 and current 2026); sensitivity runs (eta 0.5, Sy/K ends, +/-20% rain) — [METH] lines 299-316.
- Calibration/validation: logger-based (RMSE, bias, NSE; leave-one-station-out), spatial pattern vs Sentinel-1 soil-moisture anomaly and InSAR rank correlation, fire outcome AUC on held-out years, early warning lead time, before-after-control-impact; Monte Carlo >=200 runs incl. correlated DTM error; GLUE-style equifinality handling; Monte Carlo "precomputed offline per KHG and scenario and cached" — [METH] lines 318-340.
- Dashboard: KHG selector, parameter panel with registry snapshot, scenario builder, priority sliders; map layers (water table depth, deficit/surplus, risk class, P(>0.4 m), burn history, infrastructure), canal flow view, time series modelled vs observed, ranked block and pump lists, S0-S5 comparison; six headline indicators (% KHG meeting 0.4 m target, area-days deeper than 0.4 m, risk-weighted remaining deficit m3, ha rewetted per block, water pumped and fuel, high/extreme-risk area left); implementation suggestion Python backend + Streamlit/Panel + leafmap/pydeck — [METH] lines 342-372, 449-453.

(2020) siting method; repeat-neraca's solver benchmark reproduces the canal rise exactly (100.2 m and 206.6 m for 5/10 blocks) and the optimised/random improvement ratios only with transmissivity x100 (about 8x and 7x vs ~7x in the paper); the shipped code could not reproduce its stored results (unit inconsistency) — [D08] lines 57-87.

The plan's compartment-scale, canal-graph, gate/pump-coupled hydrology is the largest unbuilt block, and its prerequisites (canal network module, compartment delineation, logger re-referencing) are also unbuilt in shared form.
- Re-peat's definition of water sharing needs: canal/reach levels over time, gate operating states, pump flows and relay travel time, none of which have data sources; neraca's own finding (managed levels, outside water) implies the allocation model's central inputs are management data not satellite data.
- A satellite-based operational system would plug into Re-peat at: forcing (C4), water-table proxy/validation (replacing or supplementing SiTMAT), fire/burn layers (prioritisation), moisture raster (pump `moisture` input; CPWI from Sentinel-2/1), and the dashboard hazard classes. The plan documents anticipate S1/InSAR only as validation/check layers.
- `repeat-pump` already contains its own sekat siting, routing and 2D groundwater optimiser using a port of the neraca testbed solver (duplication risk: the same solver in two repos with a NOTICE for Urzainki 2020).

### Gaps
- No concrete operational deployment plan, scheduling cadence, forecast data source selection, hosting target, user-access model, or SLA is documented. The only fixed dates are a single 2026 dry season and the dry-season window defaults (1 June-31 Oct).
- No acceptance criteria for neraca beyond `neraca.min_spatial_skill` (Spearman >= 0.3 and RMSE below no-skill baseline) and the sanity-test list in [METH] lines 534-544 (unchecked boxes, not implemented for Tier 1).
- Which agency (KLH/BPLH or BPGMN) is the primary user, and whether Tier 2 is in scope, are open questions in [METH] lines 431-434.

## 5. TODOs, open issues, limitations, roadmap items relevant to hydrology/water balance

### Takeaway
The hub is unusually explicit about limitations. The binding ones for water balance: terrain accuracy (metres vs decimetre heads, paused pending LiDAR), one-season/concession-dominated water-table data, 25 km forcing, a physical model that fails spatial skill and cannot hold managed levels, boreal peat hydraulic tables, constant Sy, unmeasured block leakage/conveyance losses, missing canal/gate/pump inventories, and unresolved parameter drift between docs and registry.

### Cited Findings

**Terrain**
- DEMNAS published accuracy RMSE 2.2-5.0 m; smoothed DTMs 1.2-2.8 m; relative error at 1-2 km 1.1-2.6 m RMS (provisional) vs 0.3 m block step; "block siting ... not supportable from the DTM alone", direction of canal flow only where slope exceeds error; pump lift needs surveyed levels — [D08] lines 11, 17-24; [METH] lines 73, 420. Blocker: "paused pending airborne LiDAR"; Sebangau open land sits ~4 m below lidar ground, unexplained — [D-README] line 65; `GH\repeat-dem\AGENTS.md` line 16. Datum errors of 20-30 cm "as large as the signal" — [METH] line 499. Subsidence 2.2 cm/yr mean, 4-5 under plantations (epoch shift needed) — [METH] line 70; [D05] lines 143-144.
- Input-raster quality: DTM and peat-depth valid on only ~47-54% of sample grids; peat-depth floor at 50 cm; block spacing rule saturates at the 2,000 m cap in 11 of 25 sub-KHG (Kampar-Gaung) and 4 of 28 (Sugihan-Lumpur) because canal slope < 0.0002 — [KG]\README.md lines 115-119; [SL]\README.md lines 77-80.

**Hydrology/neraca**
- Physical model: canals behave as passive drains with T ~0.5 m2/day surface falling to ~0 by 0.6 m (boreal table), exchange time "years", every free cell the same curve; scaling T up deepens the simulated water table; Sugihan-Lumpur bias +23 cm (sim 66 vs obs 43), correlation 0.96; Kampar-Gaung bias -17 cm, r 0.68; ET stress from PEATCLSM-Trop thresholds helps only marginally — `neraca-air.md` "First results" items 1-5.
- Bucket model: "Neraca air does not pass": spatial Spearman -0.03/-0.02 (Kampar-Gaung), out-of-fold RMSE 16.5 cm worse than 15.3 cm no-skill; fit hits grid edge; Sugihan-Lumpur error 11.3 cm vs 5.7 cm baseline, spatial Spearman 0.23/-0.07; a drainage-term selection rule "designed for Kampar-Gaung and should be revisited" — [KG]\README.md lines 98-107; [SL]\README.md lines 59-66.
- Needs (stated): "Neraca air needs rainfall and evaporation finer than 25 km, and information on water management (weirs, canal blocks, concession practice), before it can say which sub-KHG dry out more" — [KG]\README.md line 132; same sentence logic in `data_sources.md`.
- Parameter issues: Sy 0.15 constant for the deficit though methodology uses depth-dependent Sy; peat tables boreal; `k_forest/k_drained` registry values (30/5) "not wired into the solver"; doc vs registry drift on L_inf burnt, K, ET (see Q3 item 13); no measured block leakage, relay conveyance loss, deep well yield; runoff coefficient from 2 wet months at one site; canal influence on burnt peat from one site/4 months — [D05] lines 162-169; [D08] line 99; `neraca-air.md` Not done 3.
- Sanity tests of the methodology (water balance closure within 1% per compartment, no-rain decline 1.0-2.2 cm/day, no-block monotonicity, submerged-weir continuity, horizon-1 LP reproduces plain LP, zero-DTM-error Monte Carlo collapses) are unticked and, per [D09] line 89, "belong to M2 and M3" — [METH] lines 534-544; [D09].

### Inferences
- The most direct gaps relevant to an operational satellite-based water-sharing analysis are all stated by Re-peat itself: (a) finer than 25 km rain/ET; (b) management data (canal levels, gates, pumps); (c) multi-season in-situ water tables; (d) a canal network/compartment module; (e) a terrain source adequate for decimetre heads; (f) forecast forcing; (g) a dashboard/service layer. Items outside Re-peat's own list: InSAR and Sentinel-1 water-table products as operational inputs (only experiments or addendum), soil-moisture products, river stage/tide.
- Re-peat has already tried and recorded negative results on: bucket water balance with ERA5 (no spatial skill), Sentinel-1 across-station ranking (concession signal), concession polygons from GFW (not retrievable/licensed), CHIRPS (403), DEMNAS-only siting. A gap analysis should not recommend these as new without noting the prior outcome.
