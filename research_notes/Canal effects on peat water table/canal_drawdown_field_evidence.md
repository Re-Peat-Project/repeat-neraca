# Canal drawdown and water table beside canals in tropical peat: field evidence and drainage theory

Research note, 2026-10-30. Builds on `canal_levels_evidence.md`, `groundwater_models_review.md` and `groundwater_data_papers.md`; only new items or corrections are listed.

Labels: **full text** (PDF or HTML downloaded, text extracted and read by me), **abstract** (abstract page read), **snippet** (search-tool summary only, not verified against the paper). Numbers marked "arithmetic" are my own division or subtraction of cited values.

Tool limits this session: the Scite tools returned "paid plan required", so none of its full text was used. Publisher sites for Ismail et al. 2021 (Oulu, IWA), the Springer Block C chapter and the 2025 West Kalimantan conference paper returned 403 or a login redirect. Searches for Taufik, Apers, SIPALAGA and SATREPS station-to-canal distance analyses found nothing (see Gaps).

Short answer for the Mendahara-Batanghari conflict: of everything read, no source shows a water table that is shallower near an open, unblocked canal than far from it in the dry season. The sources split into three cases: (1) unblocked, draining canals give a deeper water table near the canal (Ritzema 2014, clearest numbers); (2) canals that are dry or held shallow give no gradient or a reversed one (Putra et al. 2021); (3) depth below the local surface can be smaller near canals because the ground there is lower (Sutikno et al. 2019, Ritzema 2014 Fig. 8), which is a depth-versus-elevation effect, not a source effect.

## 1. What do field transects perpendicular to canals show for water table depth versus distance?

### Takeaway
The only transect with a clean, repeated distance profile from an unblocked canal is Ritzema et al. 2014 (Kalampangan Canal, Central Kalimantan): in the dry season the water table is 0.88 m deep at 10 m, 0.54 m at 100 m and 0.33 m at 1000 m; in the rainy season 0.50, 0.13 and 0.06 m. Transects on blocked or restored canals (Sungai Tohor, Dompas) show the canal level pushing the peat water table over about 100 to 200 m. Outside the restoration setting (oil palm, Acacia, rice scheme), no tropical transect with numbers at tens of metres was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Ritzema et al. 2014, Kalampangan Canal, ex-Mega Rice Project, Central Kalimantan (full text).** Four transects, 11 observation wells on each side of the canal up to 500 m (T1, T2 degraded; T3, T4 natural forest); T3 extended 3 km to the Sebangau River and 7.5 km to the Kahayan River with wells every 500 m; monitoring Sept 2004 to June 2009. Table 4 (average of T1 to T4, Sept 2004 to Apr 2007), water table depth in m below surface at distance from canal:

  | Distance | 10 m | 100 m | 400 m | 1000 m |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Dry season (Jul-Oct) | -0.88 | -0.54 | -0.35 | -0.33 |
  | Rainy season (Nov-Apr) | -0.50 | -0.13 | -0.05 | -0.06 |
  | Whole year | -0.56 | -0.24 | -0.12 | -0.15 |

  Text: "the water table close to the canals was 0.40-0.50 m deeper compared to the water table at a distance of 1000 m away from the canal ... This difference is consistent throughout the year, although in the dry season, the water tables are, on average, 0.30 m deeper than in the rainy season." Source: Ritzema, H., Limin, S., Kusin, K., Jauhiainen, J., Wösten, H. (2014). Catena 114, 11-20. PDF: https://luk.tsipil.ugm.ac.id/rawa/PLG/1-s2.0-S0341816213002531-main.pdf
- Same paper, Table 5 and 6: the canal cuts the dome flow, so the water table on the downstream (Sebangau) side is deeper than on the dome (Kahayan) side. Dry season: degraded 0.84 m vs 0.52 m, natural forest 1.04 m vs 0.62 m; rainy season: degraded 0.25 vs 0.03 m, forest 0.45 vs 0.19 m (full text, same PDF). The canal "eats itself into the peat dome", with deeper water tables, higher subsidence and a depression at the canal (Fig. 8, text).
- Same paper, dams on the canal: in the first months after construction (2005) the average water table along T1, T2, T3 was 0.26-0.35 m below ground, against 1.02-1.22 m before the dams; in the following dry season (July-November 2006) it dropped again to "well below 0.40" m (full text). Dams are not watertight; seepage through peat means "water tables in the surrounding peat [can drop] as low as 1.0 m below the surface in the dry season".
- **Sutikno et al. 2019, Sungai Tohor, Pulau Tebing Tinggi, Riau (full text; the test KHG area).** Five dipwells at 20, 70, 120, 170 and 220 m from a canal 4 m wide and 3 m deep, blocks 600 m apart, secondary peat swamp forest, peat 4 to 5 m, experiment July 2018 (dry season), logging every 5 min. Raising canal level by 0.5 m raised the water table 0.41 m at 20 m and 0.2 m at 70 m, with no response at 120, 170, 220 m. A second closure raised the canal 0.69 m and the water table 0.63, 0.29, 0.13 and 0.04 m at 20, 70, 120, 170 m (no response at 220 m). Authors: effect radius about 170 m, "the ground surface around the canal tends to be lower, and it is higher gradually from canal block toward SP-05". Source: Sutikno, S. et al. (2019). MATEC Web of Conferences 276, 06003. https://doi.org/10.1051/matecconf/201927606003 (PDF: https://luk.tsipil.ugm.ac.id/rawa/2019SigitSutikno-CanalBlockingRiauTropicalPeatland.pdf). Arithmetic: transmitted fraction of the 0.69 m canal rise is 0.91 at 20 m, 0.42 at 70 m, 0.19 at 120 m, 0.06 at 170 m.
- **Alfath, Rinaldi & Sutikno 2020, Dompas village, Bengkalis, Riau (full text, Indonesian).** Four transects, four deep wells each, before and after canal blocking; abstract: GWL raised 10 to 21 cm with "wetting distance further than 75 meters". Rise at 0.5 / 25.5 / 50.5 / 75.5 m: T1 10.7/11.9/12.3/12.0 cm; T2 21/10.3/10.8/13.7; T3 15.5/14.3/12.3/13.7; T4 13.9/14.9/14.8/13.8. The rise is flat across 0.5 to 75.5 m; wells stop at 75.5 m so the decay length is not observed. Rise upstream and also downstream of the block (authors suggest seepage under and around the block). Source: Jom FTEKNIK 7(1), Jan-Jun 2020. https://jom.unri.ac.id/index.php/JOMFTEKNIK/article/download/26505/25624
- **Hooijer et al. 2024, former Acacia plantation next to forest, Sembilang area, South Sumatra (full text).** 7.5-year mean water table depth by zone along the 4.2 km transect from the main road canal, zones 700 m wide (mean of 2 to 5 dipwells each): 0-700 m -0.63; 700-1400 m -0.52; 1400-2100 m -0.36; 2100-2800 m -0.35; 2800-3500 m -0.24; 3500-4200 m -0.25. El Niño year mid-2019 to mid-2020: -0.87, -0.83, -0.64, -0.45, -0.40, -0.36. The 10 to 90 percentile range was "consistently around 0.4 m" in all zones. Forest more than 1400 m from the perimeter canal: mean -0.25 m, median -0.23 m. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-60462-3 (Sci Rep 14, 10721). The gradient here is over kilometres with 700 m resolution, so it says nothing about the first 50 m. This is the only plantation/restoration site with a distance gradient and it is a blocked, retired plantation.
- **Putra, Holden & Baird 2021, Sebangau (Tumbang Nusa "Drained" site, degraded and burned peat with unblocked ditches) (full text).** Wet period: mean water table depth 37.2 cm (SD 9.5) in wells near ditches (AA1, AA3, mean distance to surrounding ditches 21 m and 29 m), 20.5 cm (SD 6.7) in the well farthest from ditches (AA2, 173 m). Wet-season interquartile range at Drained 22.5 to 41.3 cm. Water level in the nearest well was "similar to the ditch water level" in the wet period. In the dry period the well-minus-ditch difference was under 10 cm at all sites. https://doi.org/10.1002/hyp.14174 (PDF: https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/179900/1/hyp.14174.pdf). This is the closest match to the Mendahara situation in structure (small ditches, degraded land), and it shows drawdown near ditches in the wet season and no gradient in the dry season.
- **Kurnianto et al. 2026, Pulau Padang (full text, model, not field).** Drainage impact on the adjacent forest decays as a power law with distance from the outermost canal: 9 cm at 100 m, below 5 cm at about 300 m with dams; 19 cm at 100 m and beyond 300 m without dams; "these effects never approached zero, even at 1 km", and the below-5 cm level equals the model RMSE. https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541143/
- **Sinclair et al. 2020 (abstract).** Degraded peat sampled 50-200 m from canals versus 300+ m; "sites farther from canals had less intense water table drawdowns". No water table numbers on the page. Sci Total Environ 699, 134199. https://research.usc.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Effects-of-distance-from-canal-and/99451468802621
- **Ismail et al. 2021, Pulau Padang (abstract via search tool, snippet).** Large-scale plantation drainage: water table about -1.8 m, recession up to 3.5 cm/day; farm-scale drains near villages: shallower, recession up to 1.8 cm/day; Sy 0.1 at -1.5 m versus up to 0.3 near the top; "proximity to drainages extended discharge flow to deeper layers". Hydrology Research 52(6), 1372-1388. https://doi.org/10.2166/nh.2021.062 (full text not reached).
- **Ismail, Karyanto & Kløve 2018 (EGU abstract, snippet).** Degraded Central Kalimantan peat: dry-season drawdown from drainage "reaches a minimum value three kilometers away from the ditch" provided the surface elevation does not fall below that near the main canals. https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2018/EGU2018-17323.pdf
- **Evans et al. 2019 (full text; already in project notes).** Subsidence under Acacia unrelated to distance to nearest canal (R2 = 0.00, p = 0.34); field drains not included, "true distance to the nearest drainage feature may be less than (and not necessarily related to) distance to the nearest canal". This is subsidence, not water table.
- Other snippets, not verified: Santosa & Qirom (UPR, Tumbang Nusa): 17 water table points over a 4 km transect at 250 m, water table related to elevation and distance from canal (https://e-journal.upr.ac.id/index.php/Agp/article/view/12603; numbers not seen). A 2025 conference paper on burned peat in West Kalimantan: response to a GWL rise is quicker at 20 m from the canal and starts to decrease at about 70 m (https://www.bio-conferences.org/10.1051/bioconf/202516703013; 403). UGM thesis, Central Kalimantan: April-July decline 0.13 to 1.06 m in downstream canals and 0.14 to 0.75 m in upstream canals (https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/home/detail_pencarian_downloadfiles/875400).

### Inferences
- For unblocked, draining canals in open or degraded peat the field profile has two scales: a steep part within about 100 m (Ritzema: 0.21 m deeper than at 1000 m at 100 m in the dry season, 0.07 m in the rainy season; arithmetic) and a long tail that is within about 0.02 m of the 1000 m value at 400 m (dry) and 0.01 m (rainy).
- The model's 41 cm within 50 m versus 23 cm beyond 1 km (difference 18 cm) is smaller than Ritzema's dry-season contrast (0.88 vs 0.33 m, 55 cm) and in the same direction. Its direction agrees with the unblocked-canal literature; the stations' direction does not.
- Depth below the local surface can be shallower near a canal when the ground has subsided there (Ritzema Fig. 8; Sutikno: ground lower around the canal). Check whether station "depth" is relative to the local surface or to a DEM, and whether the model's DEM carries the subsided margin.

### Gaps
- No tropical transect with water table versus distance from a drain for oil palm, Acacia or a rice scheme (e.g. Pulau Padang plantation, Sumatran smallholder oil palm) was found with numbers at tens of metres. Ritzema 2014 canal water level at the time of the transects is not in the text I extracted.
- Ritzema's 1000 m value comes from the extended T3 line (wells every 500 m); the table averages T1 to T4 and does not state how many wells make up the 400 and 1000 m columns.
- Ismail 2021 full text, Santosa & Qirom numbers, the West Kalimantan 2025 paper and the Springer Block C chapter were not readable.
- Malaysian transects: none found.

## 2. How does the drawdown depend on canal level, canal spacing, peat conductivity, season and recent rain? Is influence only tens to a few hundred metres?

### Takeaway
Canal level, season and rain all show up in the data, canal spacing and conductivity mostly through design theory and models. Influence distance depends on what is asked: the effect of a change in canal level reaches 100 to 200 m (Sutikno, Dompas); the drainage drawdown of an unblocked dome reaches several hundred metres at the 0.02 m level (Ritzema) and kilometres at the few-centimetre level (Hooijer 2024, Ismail EGU 2018, Kurnianto model). "Only tens to a few hundred metres" holds for level changes at the canal, not for the whole drainage effect.

### Cited Findings
- Canal level: a 0.69 m canal rise gave 0.63 m at 20 m and 0.04 m at 170 m (Sutikno 2019, full text; arithmetic transmitted fractions 0.91, 0.42, 0.19, 0.06 at 20, 70, 120, 170 m). A 0.5 m rise gave 0.41 and 0.2 m at 20 and 70 m. The authors state the water table at 170 m already stood at the elevation of the raised canal water, so the reach was capped by topography.
- Season: Ritzema Table 4 (above), dry-season water table 0.30 m deeper on average, and the near-far contrast 0.55 m (dry) versus 0.44 m (rainy) between 10 and 1000 m (arithmetic). Putra 2021 (full text): well-ditch differences under 10 cm in the dry period at all sites because ditches were dry; 11.9 to 31.0 cm at Drained in the wet period.
- Recent rain: at Sungai Tohor dipwells SP-03 and SP-04 (120 and 170 m) "were more affected by rainfall than that of canal blocking"; three rain days gave a small effect at the canal and SP-01/02 and a significant effect at SP-03/04 (Sutikno 2019, full text). At Sebangau, ditch dams "depend on rainfall inputs and also ditch water levels downstream of" the dams, and in the dry season dams did not raise the water table because the ditches were dry (Putra 2021, full text). Hydraulic gradient from the lowest ditch outlet to the farthest well, interquartile range: 3.7e-4 to 7.8e-4 m/m at Blocked, 1.9e-3 to 2.6e-3 m/m at Drained (abstract page of Putra 2021).
- Canal spacing: Sutikno's canal had blocks 600 m apart and canal 4 m wide by 3 m deep (full text). Kurnianto (full text) models 100 m cells with dams; Putra, Baird & Holden 2022 (DigiBog_Hydro, snippet): in the dry season of any ENSO scenario ditch dams alone had significant effects only within 26 m (moderately degraded) and 12 m (severely degraded) of a ditch (model, https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/188257).
- Conductivity: see existing notes (Hooijer 2024 K 119 m/d; Kurnianto Ksat 8.6-173 m/d; Baird 2017). New: Ritzema 2014 attributes dams failing to hold dry-season water tables to "the high permeability of the peat which results in lateral seepage along and below the dams" (full text).
- Ritzema's drainage design paper (Alterra-ILRI, "The role of drainage in the wise use of tropical peatlands"; full text, undated in text read): peat has "very high infiltration rate, storage capacity and permeability", so excess rain leaves as interflow and groundwater runoff; recommends closely spaced shallow drains with control structures; the water management system occupies 15 to 20% of the area compared to under 5% on mineral soil; dome slope under 0.5 m/km in the centre and over 2 m/km at the edges, so structures 1 to 2 km apart at the centre. https://edepot.wur.nl/386
- Long-reach evidence: Hooijer 2024 (above) gradient over about 3 km; mean subsidence in forest 1400-2100 m from canals fell from 1.6 to 0.7 cm/yr after rewetting, "drainage impact can extend far from canals"; Ismail EGU 2018 (snippet) 3 km; Kurnianto 2026 (model) not zero at 1 km.

### Inferences
- With the Sutikno decay (91% at 20 m, 6% at 170 m of a canal change) the scale length is roughly 60 to 80 m for a 4 m wide, 3 m deep canal in forest on 4 to 5 m peat (my estimate from the four points, not a published value). A model with 100 m cells cannot represent that decay; it needs a sub-grid ditch resistance (see section 5).
- Seasonal contrast matters for Mendahara: stations compared in the dry season against canals that are dry or low would show no near-canal drawdown (Putra), while wet-season data show it.

### Gaps
- No field study varies canal spacing at fixed other conditions. No sweep of drawdown against K at tropical sites.
- Sutikno 2019 gives the rise, not the pre-block water table depths or K at the wells.

## 3. Under what conditions do canals not drain the adjacent peat (abandoned, silted, vegetation-choked, blocked, dry in the dry season)? Any quantification?

### Takeaway
Two quantified cases: ditches that are dry in the dry season (Putra 2021) and fully blocked canals that become vegetated (Hooijer 2024, open water down to 26% on average). No survey gives the share of canals with water in the dry season, nor canal water level relative to peat water table at network scale.

### Cited Findings
- Sebangau, Tumbang Nusa Drained and Blocked sites (Putra 2021, full text): "In the dry season, the ditches were dry and water did not flow to them"; "Prior to November 2019, water levels of zero at the ditch bed were recorded as all ditches were dry". "The drainage effect was barely perceptible during the late dry season, as the ditch water levels were at least on or below the ditch bed ... lateral flow loss from the peat mass into ditches did not exist during this period"; evapotranspiration alone lowered levels (drawdown 9.3 mm/d forested, 9.6 blocked, 12.7 drained, dry season, abstract). Dry-season water tables at all three sites were deeper than 40 cm.
- Same paper: wet-period ditch water height at Blocked downstream of the dam averaged 78.7 cm (SD 40.4) and at Drained Main ditch 3 38.6 cm (SD 35.4), heights above the ditch bed.
- Fully blocked canals without bypasses (Hooijer 2024, full text): rapid aquatic vegetation growth, open water area decreased to 26% on average by 2022-2023 (range 7 to 45%); canals with bypasses stayed far less covered; all dams built 2015-2017 were intact 6 to 8 years later; bypasses near the main road canal eroded.
- Dams leak: Ritzema 2014 (full text) dams on Kalampangan canal damaged by seepage underneath and along the sides in Dec 2005 and Aug 2006; Kalampangan Canal dams (no spillway) built in waterlogged conditions.
- Canal level versus peat level in managed Sumatran plantations is covered in the existing note (Hooijer 2024, Vernimmen & Hooijer 2026: canal under 0.1 m below peat water table); not repeated.
- Snippets, unverified: a South Kalimantan paper attributes dry peat canals to siltation and sedimentation because the canals were not designed to suit the peat contours (https://ppjp.ulm.ac.id/journals/index.php/jss/article/view/3953/0, Liang Anggang; abstract-level only). A Sriwijaya University thesis at a Jambi plantation reports a correlation of 0.931 between groundwater table height and canal water level (https://rama.kemdiktisaintek.go.id/document/detail/oai:repository.unsri.ac.id:95930-24).

### Inferences
- A canal with no dry-season water exerts no lateral drawdown on that day; the dry-season water table then follows ET and position (Putra). This is a candidate explanation for a stationary near-canal water table that is shallower than the model's.
- The stage-fixed-at-0.8/0.3 m assumption fails exactly where canals are dry, vegetation-choked or perched above the peat water table.

### Gaps
- No quantified share of dry canals, no vegetation or sedimentation survey, no canal water level versus peat water table at network scale for Sumatra or Kalimantan outside Hooijer's plantations.
- The Liang Anggang siltation statement and the Jambi correlation thesis were not read.

## 4. Is there evidence that the water table near canals can be shallower than away from canals (canals as sources, flooding, ponding)?

### Takeaway
Only in situations where the canal water is held up: blocked canals (Sutikno, Dompas, Putra "Blocked"), the dome side of a canal that intercepts flow (Ritzema), and low plantation canals at 1 to 3 m elevation (existing note). No source shows an open, unblocked canal acting as a water source to the nearby peat in the dry season. For depth below the surface, subsided ground beside canals gives a second route. No Taufik, Apers, SIPALAGA, SATREPS or Mendeley analysis of station distance to canal was found.

### Cited Findings
- Blocked canal as source: canal raised 0.69 m raised peat water table 0.63 m at 20 m (Sutikno 2019, full text). At Dompas the rise after blocking was 10 to 21 cm at 0.5 to 75.5 m (Alfath et al. 2020, full text).
- Ponding near ditches at a blocked site: at Blocked "a large portion ... was ponded in the wet period, especially near ditches, and the inundation height reached 23 cm"; inundation up to 23 cm at Blocked, wet-period interquartile range 7.9 cm above to 10.2 cm below the surface (Putra 2021, full text). In contrast at the unblocked Drained site, wells near ditches were deeper (37.2 cm) than the far well (20.5 cm).
- Ditch higher than peat at the centre during the transition: "ditch water levels rose more quickly than peat water levels at the centre of Drained" (well AA2 lower than the ditch level) (Putra 2021, full text).
- Dome-side shallower: water table on the dome side of the canal 0.17 to 0.30 m deep against 0.41 to 0.62 m on the downstream side (Ritzema 2014 Table 5, full text), because of interception of overland and interflow by the canal.
- Surface lower near canals: "The ground surface around the canal tends to be lower" (Sutikno 2019); the canal has "depressions" from higher subsidence near canals, Fig. 8 (Ritzema 2014). Existing note: Vernimmen et al. 2019, drops of more than 1 m within 500 m of plantation boundary canals.
- Unrelated to distance: Evans 2019 (R2 = 0.00 for subsidence versus distance to canal, field drains excluded).
- Hooijer 2024 (full text): canals with blocks 0.5 m higher on the east side; near the main road canal the water table is the deepest of the transect (-0.63 m), the opposite of a canal source effect.

### Inferences
- The four of five Mendahara stations within 60 m of a canal reading 13 to 35 cm are consistent with: (a) canals dry or low at those places in the dry season (Putra), (b) held water or blocks (Sutikno), (c) local subsidence making the surface close to the water table, (d) canal-adjacent stations being sited on low ground or in wet corners. The published data do not separate these; (a) and (c) are the ones with measured numbers.
- A model that fixes canal depth below the DEM at all times will produce drawdown in the dry season wherever the real canal is dry; a floor of "canal level not below water table" or a seasonal canal level is closer to Putra's observation.

### Gaps
- Not found: any analysis of SIPALAGA/SATREPS/SiTMAT stations by distance to the nearest canal (Taufik 2022 and 2023, Apers 2022). The Scite tools were unavailable and general searches returned no such analysis.
- Hirano, Page, Jauhiainen, Hooijer 2012, Wösten 2008, Sumarga 2016 and Ritzema's other papers were searched but not read for canal-distance data; the Wösten 2008 and Sumarga searches returned no relevant text.

## 5. Classical ditch drainage theory: profile, ditch resistance and entrance resistance for narrow canals in coarse cells

### Takeaway
Hooghoudt's equation relates the mid-spacing water table height to flow, K, spacing and an equivalent depth that absorbs convergence (radial resistance) near the drain; the ditch is represented by a radius equal to wetted perimeter over pi. Entrance resistance is an extra term handled by an adjusted equation (Oosterbaan). For MODFLOW-type models the equivalent is the drain or river conductance, which has to be adjusted when the grid size changes. The full entrance-resistance formula was not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Hooghoudt (Wikipedia "Drainage equation", page read): Q L^2 = 8 Kb d (Dd - Dw) + 4 Ka (Dd - Dw)^2, where Kb and Ka are the conductivities below and above drain level, Dd drain depth, Dw midway water table depth, L spacing, d equivalent depth. Closed-form equivalent depth: d = pi L / 8 {ln(L / (pi r)) + F(x)}, x = 2 pi (Di - Dd) / L, F(x) = sum over n = 1, 3, 5, ... of 4 e^(-2 n x) / (n (1 - e^(-2 n x))), where Di is the depth of the impermeable layer. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_equation
- Ditch as drain (search-tool summary of waterlog.info Drainage equation notes, snippet): "the drain is treated as a pipe with an equivalent radius calculated as R = W/pi, where W is the wetted perimeter"; Hooghoudt's equation "can be adjusted for entrance resistance using the principles discussed by Oosterbaan et al. (1989, 1990)", adjusted equation in Oosterbaan's sloping-land paper; EnDrain handles entrance resistance and layered anisotropic soils (https://waterlog.info/pdf/sloping.pdf; fetch failed, host not resolved).
- Tropical peat design practice (Ritzema, Alterra-ILRI, full text): steady-state Hooghoudt is suitable for Borneo's humid climate and used to calculate drain spacing, with a water table criterion (average wet season) setting spacing and a discharge criterion (e.g. one-in-5-year storm) setting drain capacity; structures with small head differences because peat is so permeable. https://edepot.wur.nl/386
- MODFLOW (search-tool summaries of USGS ModelMuse help, snippet): drain or river conductance = K L W / M (boundary K, length in cell, width, thickness); "grid cell size changes the conductance" and head is averaged over a different area (Mehl & Hill 2010, as cited); MODFLOW 6 drain option DDRN ramps conductance from 0 at drain elevation to full over a head interval. https://water.usgs.gov/nrp/gwsoftware/ModelMuse/Help/drn_object_pane.html. The USGS MODFLOW-2000 drain page read by fetch gives only the definition "hydraulic conductance of the interface between the aquifer and the drain" and the zero-flux threshold.

### Inferences
- Hooghoudt's form has drawdown above drain level proportional to L^2 and inversely proportional to K times an effective depth d, which is why very permeable peat (K around 100 m/d per the existing notes) carries low drawdown between closely spaced drains and why the canal level, not K, sets the water table in managed plantations.
- For a 100 m model cell with a 1 to 10 m ditch, the cell-average head is not the head at the ditch. The standard handling is an extra resistance (conductance) between cell and canal derived from the radial term in the equivalent depth with r = W/pi. I did not find a published tropical peat value for this resistance, nor a published implementation for 100 m cells.
- Ritzema's argument that very permeable peat gives "interflow" drainage explains why drainage influence can exceed 100 m even for narrow canals.

### Gaps
- The explicit entrance-resistance expression (We or h_entry) and the Dupuit/ellipse profile were not fetched (waterlog.info unreachable; the Wikipedia page gave no formula). The profile shape claim is not sourced and is left out.
- No tropical-peat paper that applies Hooghoudt or Oosterbaan entrance resistance with measured ditch levels and wells was found; no Malaysian transect or drain-spacing experiment (Sarawak DID 2001 guidelines are cited by Ritzema but were not found online).
