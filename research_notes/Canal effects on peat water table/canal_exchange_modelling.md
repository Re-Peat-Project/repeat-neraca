## 1. How the reference models couple canals to peat (conductance, stage as state or fixed, blocks, initial and bounding conditions)

### Takeaway
Urzainki 2023 and Kurnianto 2026 both treat canal stage as a state solved in a 1D channel network, with exchange carried by the Darcy flow of the peat cell next to the canal (Urzainki) or by a calibrated leakage coefficient (MIKE Hydro). Putra 2022 (DigiBog) uses fixed Dirichlet ditch levels per 100-day period. None of the three uses a sub-cell geometric factor like ours. The MODFLOW 6 conductance formulas could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Urzainki 2023: canal stage solved with a diffusive wave, mass balance `∂h/∂t = −(1/B)∂Q/∂x + q/B`, momentum `∂h/∂x = −n²Q|Q|/(A²R^(4/3))`; rectangular canals, one width B for every node; Manning n rises as the canal level falls (n ≈ 0.055 for a full canal); canal grid Δx = 50 m — [Urzainki 2023, Sect. 2.2.1 and Appendix A](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/20/2099/2023/) (*full text*, first 100,000 characters of the page; Table 1 values, B and z and K_b, were not in the extracted text).
- Urzainki 2023 exchange: the peat module (2D, Eq. 5 `Sy(h) ∂h/∂t = ∇·T(h)∇h + P − ET`) does not set canal level. The head change in canal cells during the peat step gives a lateral inflow q per unit canal length, used as a source or sink in the next canal step. Canal-to-canal faces get T = 0; canal-to-peat faces keep T ≠ 0. "No separate seepage or conductance coefficient is described" — [same](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/20/2099/2023/) (*full text*).
- Urzainki 2023, ponding: when canal level exceeds the ground, "the volume of ponding water was distributed instantaneously throughout the corresponding cell area in the PHM step"; no separate overland-flow routine in the extracted text — [same](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/20/2099/2023/) (*full text*).
- Urzainki 2023, boundaries and initial state: no-flow Neumann at all canal boundary nodes including the catchment outlet; peat fixed head ζ = −0.2 m at domain boundaries; initial WTD from saturation, run 50 days at ET 7.5 mm/d with blocks, pick the day best matching dipwells; time step 1/24 d (1/100 d on non-convergence) — [same](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/20/2099/2023/) (*full text*).
- Urzainki 2023, coupling is not mass conserving. Author response: "The coupling between the PHM and CNM approximates water balance, but it does not strictly conserve mass", attributed to any model that solves groundwater and surface water in separate modules; the error was not estimated. Blocks are watertight barriers from surface to the impermeable base, "while in reality they are relatively porous" — [Author comment bg-2022-218 (AC1)](https://bg.copernicus.org/preprints/bg-2022-218/bg-2022-218-ATC1.pdf) (*full text*, extracted locally). I found no statement in it of an error in the peat module mathematics; the lead in `groundwater_models_review.md` section 2 item 6 is not confirmed or refuted by this file.
- Kurnianto 2026 (MIKE SHE + MIKE Hydro): canal and river levels are 1D MIKE Hydro features; "River links, automatically generated from MIKE Hydro channel coordinates, define the hydraulic interface between channel reaches and adjacent MIKE SHE grid cells". Each step Hydro passes stage to the links and SHE returns overland and groundwater exchange fluxes; "channel overflow and reverse flow occur whenever simulated water levels exceed local bank elevations". Upstream channel ends: constant no-flow; downstream ends: measured tide. 2014 used as warm-up for initial conditions — [Kurnianto 2026, Sect. 2.3](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541143/1/N541143JA.pdf) (*full text*, PDF text extracted).
- Kurnianto 2026 parameters (Table 1): MIKE Hydro bed resistance 0.04 s m^-1/3 (Arcement and Schneider method); leakage coefficient 1.00 × 10^-4 (range 5 × 10^-5 to 2 × 10^-4), unit printed as "(-)", "calibrated in the model". Dams: 123 full-width broad-crested weirs, crest 0.6 m below adjacent ground — [same](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541143/1/N541143JA.pdf) (*full text*).
- Putra 2022 (DigiBog_Hydro, 1 m cells): ditch water levels are Dirichlet boundaries, constant within each of four ~100-day periods (Late Wet, Early Dry, Late Dry, Early Wet) with 12-day overlaps, values from field data and forest rangers; previous period's water levels are the next initial condition. Dams act by changing those set levels. Dry-season dam effect alone reached only 26 m (moderately degraded) and 12 m (severely degraded) laterally — [Putra, Baird and Holden 2022, Hydrol Process 36, e14470, Sect. 2-3](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/188257/) (*full text* of the PDF, extracted locally; lateral distances from the abstract-level search summary, *snippet*).
- MODFLOW 6 DRN: `cond` is "the hydraulic conductance of the interface between the aquifer and the drain", `elev` the drain elevation; the optional AUXDEPTHNAME depth scales COND between ELEV and ELEV + DDRN (linear or cubic scaling). The page gives no conductance formula — [MODFLOW 6 DRN input](https://modflow6.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_mf6io/gwf-drn.html) (*full text* of the input page).
- MODFLOW 6 SFR: reach inputs RLEN, RWID, RBTH, RHK (streambed thickness and conductivity), MAN (Manning). In the SIMPLE routing status "the stage is set to the top of the reach and leakage is set equal to the sum of inflows" if calculated leakage exceeds inflows; RUNOFF is "the volumetric rate of diffuse overland runoff that enters the streamflow routing reach". SFR can be used with MVR — [MODFLOW 6 SFR input](https://modflow6.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_mf6io/gwf-sfr.html) (*full text* of the input page). The page does not say runoff is routed by MVR; it only lists a from-mvr inflow observation.
- Standard MODFLOW riverbed conductance is `Kr × L × W / M` (W stream width, L reach length in the cell, M bed thickness); it contains no aquifer property or grid size — [Cousquer et al. 2017 as quoted in a search summary](https://geosciences.univ-tours.fr/images/media/20171128143650-cousquer_et_al_2017_groundwater.pdf) (*snippet*; the MODFLOW-2005 RIV page I fetched states only "Cond — is the riverbed hydraulic conductance").

### Inferences
- Our sub-cell factor (LEAK_K (L/dx) max(1, L/dx) T) is a different construction from all three. Only DRN/RIV/SFR-type conductance and the MIKE leakage coefficient are of the same kind (head-dependent flux), and the latter is calibrated, not derived.
- Urzainki 2023 gets exchange from the cell-to-cell T of the peat grid with canals as 50 m / 1D cells. It does not correct for a canal narrower than the cell either; the width B enters only in the canal module. So it supplies no precedent for a sub-cell correction.
- Putra's fixed per-period ditch level is the closest published analogue to our "fixed level per class", with the level changed by season. Seasonal change of the fixed level is a cheap alternative to a lumped store.
- Initial stage: none of the three starts the canal from a measured stage. Urzainki spins up from saturation and picks a day by dipwells, Kurnianto spins up for a year (2014), Putra uses overlap periods. A spin-up with the outlet fixed is the common practice.

### Gaps
- MODFLOW 6 conductance formulas (SFR/RIV/DRN theory, Supplemental Technical Information chapter 3) were not retrieved, nor the Newton behaviour of DRN.
- Urzainki 2023 Table 1 and K_b value, and whether the extracted text hid an overland-flow routine in later sections (the second half of the page, beyond character 145,546, was not read).
- SIMGRO channel coupling formulas and the MIKE SHE river-link conductance formula (the Kurnianto PDF gives the coefficient but not the formula; the DHI manual was not retrieved). Whether Kurnianto's "(-)" leakage coefficient is per second is not stated.
- Whether an error in the Urzainki 2023 peat module exists (reviewer comments RC1/RC2 not read).

## 2. Exchange values for peat canals, sensitivity, and sub-cell corrections

### Takeaway
The only published tropical calibrated bed-leakage value found is Kurnianto 2026 (1 × 10^-4, range 5 × 10^-5 to 2 × 10^-4, unit unclear). No sensitivity analysis of canal conductance on peat was found. For sub-cell width, Haitjema 2005 gives an analytic recipe (leakage length λ and effective width w) and Mehl and Hill 2010 show that conductance depends on grid size, with leakage differing by up to 122%; the Hooghoudt equivalent depth is the standard treatment of radial convergence.

### Cited Findings
- Haitjema 2005 (*full text*): bed resistance `c = δ/kc`; leakage length `λ = √(kHc)` (k aquifer conductivity, H thickness); for a stream of width B represented by line-sinks on its banks, the effective width is `w = λ` if λ ≤ 0.1B, `w = λ tanh(B/2λ)` if 0.1B < λ < 2B, `w = B/2` if λ ≥ 2B; a single line-sink on the axis takes twice that width. Without a resistance parameter, shift the sink inward by `d = λ` (λ ≤ 0.1B) or `d = λ/tanh(B/2λ)`. This is derived for a 1D stream cross-section with a low-permeability bed layer — [Haitjema 2005, Dealing with Resistance to Flow into Surface Waters](https://haitjema.com/documents/Dealingwithresistancetoflowintosurfacewaters.pdf) (*full text*, extracted locally).
- Mehl and Hill 2010: stream leakage differed by up to 122% as grids were refined; "different grid resolutions require different estimated values"; conductances from coarse models or field measurements cannot be transferred to finer grids; existing grid-size criteria work for one-layer models and are inadequate for 3D; local refinement used as little as one quarter of the time and memory — [Mehl and Hill 2010, Adv Water Resour 33:430](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70037505) (*abstract*). The three conductance methods and any recommended one were not read.
- ModelMuse documentation warns that conductance depends on cell size beyond the `KLW/M` formula and may need recalibration if the grid changes — [ModelMuse RIV help, via search summary](https://water.usgs.gov/nrp/gwsoftware/ModelMuse/Help/riv_object_pane.html) (*snippet*).
- Hooghoudt equivalent depth: a reduced aquifer thickness standing in for the extra resistance of radial flow to a ditch; tables replaced by the closed form of van der Molen and Wesseling (1991); for normal recharge and drain spacing the error in maximum water-table height of Hooghoudt's formula is under 1% against a rigorous solution, largest with high recharge-to-K ratio and close drains — [search summaries of waterlog.info and an Indian water portal derivation](https://waterlog.info/lec/equation.pdf) (*snippet*).
- A steady-state Hooghoudt design for Malaysian peat fire-risk drains gave required spacings of 51-360 m (shallow peat) and 125-525 m (deep peat) depending on design drainage rate — [Malay. J. Civ. Eng. article via search summary](https://journals.utm.my/mjce/article/download/15583/7076/47474) (*snippet*).
- Calibrated leakage on tropical peat: Kurnianto 2026 range above (*full text*). No sensitivity analysis of the leakage coefficient was found in that paper's extracted text (searched for "leakage"; only Table 1 matched).
- Kurnianto 2026 does report the sensitivity that matters for blocks: dams cut drawdown below 5 cm within about 300 m of the outermost canals ([abstract](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541143/); *abstract*).

### Inferences
- Our LEAK_K = 12 comes from the horizontal-flow-limited strip solution (mean head q s²/12T). That solution has no bed resistance and no radial convergence, so in Haitjema's terms it is the λ → 0 case (w = λ small, all resistance in the aquifer). It is defensible when the bed is as permeable as the peat and the canal is much narrower than the cell, which is the situation for open canals in highly permeable peat (K 8.6-173 m/d, per the Kurnianto Table 1 range already in the notes). It will overstate exchange if canals have silted or blocked beds.
- A Hooghoudt-type radial correction adds resistance of order `(1/π) ln(a/πr)` per unit length near a canal narrower than the aquifer is thick; this was not retrieved as a sourced formula and is not recommended without checking against Haitjema or Hooghoudt tables. Our 100 m cell is large relative to peat thickness at the shallow end, so the radial term may matter there. This is an unquantified point.
- Mehl and Hill's result means LEAK_K should be checked at two cell sizes (100 m and 50 m) with the same canal; if leakage per canal length changes by tens of percent, the construction is grid-dependent. This is a test, not a published correction for our setup.

### Gaps
- Mehl and Hill's recommended conductance method and any narrow-stream correction (full text not retrieved).
- Any sensitivity analysis of canal bed conductance on tropical peat. None found in the searches run.
- Haitjema's treatment applies to a stream with a resistance layer; I found no source applying it to open peat canals.

## 3. Overland flow, ponding and runoff to canals in peat models, and runoff coefficients

### Takeaway
Our treatment (water above the surface removed) is what DigiBog does (excess above a 5 cm ponding tank lost to the boundary as "rapid overland flow") and what Urzainki 2023 approximates (ponding spread instantly over the cell). Kurnianto 2026 contradicts the idea that runoff coefficients like C = 0.12 describe overland flow: its simulated "runoff" is total island drainage (surface plus subsurface), 6% of rain in the dry year and 35% in the wet year, and it is mostly subsurface flow to canals in managed land.

### Cited Findings
- Putra 2022: surface water represented by "virtual layers"; un-bunded area allowed ponding to 5 cm; the layer has no hydraulic conductivity and "no surface runoff modelling was implemented"; rain beyond the 5 cm limit "was assumed to be immediately lost to the model boundaries. In effect, this loss is the equivalent of rapid overland flow". Bunded areas add a 30 cm high-K layer — [Putra et al. 2022, Sect. 2](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/188257/) (*full text*).
- Putra 2022 discussion: bunds "store surface water that would otherwise be lost as overland flow to the ditches", subsidising ET in the dry season; low-K peat tends to trigger surface runoff or inundation — [same](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/188257/) (*full text*).
- Kurnianto 2026: MIKE SHE solves 2D overland flow with the St. Venant finite-difference scheme; Manning n (s m^-1/3) 0.17, 0.1, 0.1, 0.05 and detention storage 0.1, 0.05, 0.05, 0.1 m across land-cover columns (forest highest roughness; unit/class mapping to columns is in Table 1 of the paper, column order not re-verified); surface and subsurface flows are routed to channels in MIKE Hydro — [Kurnianto 2026, Table 1, Sect. 2.3](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541143/1/N541143JA.pdf) (*full text*).
- Kurnianto 2026 runoff shares: peat dome forest wet year 856 mm (34% of rain), dry year 54 mm (4%); managed area wet year 880 mm (35%), dry year 89 mm (6%); entire island 98 mm (6%) dry year to 868 mm (35%) wet year; managed vs forest annual runoff 489 ± 417 vs 450 ± 448 mm, about 8% apart, small against the ~800 mm interannual swing; runoff is the second-largest term of rainfall partitioning after ET — [same, Sect. 3 results](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541143/1/N541143JA.pdf) (*full text*).
- Kurnianto 2026 mechanism: in forest the water table is near or above the surface so overland storage contributes and surface flow is a primary runoff component; in managed peat the water table stays below the surface, "GWL below the surface generates only subsurface flow and making it the predominant runoff component in both wet and dry periods" — [same, discussion](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541143/1/N541143JA.pdf) (*full text*).
- Putra (Sebangau, dry season): ditches dry, water did not flow to them; drawdown 9.3 (forested), 9.6 (blocked), 12.7 mm/d (drained without dams), attributed to ET — [search summary of Putra et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/179900/1/hyp.14174.pdf) (*snippet*; paper hyp.14174 not read).
- Other runoff-coefficient values for tropical peat: none found in this session beyond the Amal 2021/2022 records already in `runoff_coefficient_pulau_padang.md`.
- DigiBog_Hydro, PEATCLSM, SIMGRO overland treatment: not retrieved beyond Putra 2022 (above). SIMGRO and PEATCLSM overland treatment is a gap.

### Inferences
- If C = 0.12 is applied to rain and the result added to canals, it competes with the subsurface drainage the groundwater model already produces. Kurnianto's island runoff in a dry year (6% of rain) and managed-area wet year (35%) is total flow, so it brackets 0.12 only as a total, and a surface-only coefficient would double count where the grid already routes subsurface flow. This is the strongest argument against adding an SRO term to canals on top of Boussinesq flow.
- Ponded water above the surface being "removed" is the same simplification as DigiBog's tank, so the 22% of annual rain lost at one site is a modelling analogue of overland flow. If that water should reach a canal, the faithful change is to route the removed ponded depth to the nearest canal cell's store (a source term), not to add a separate coefficient. In forest and wet-season conditions Kurnianto shows surface flow is a primary runoff component, which supports routing the ponded excess; in managed land at sub-surface water tables it supports not adding anything.
- The dry-season ponding is nil, so the routing decision only affects the wet season, when canal stage is least limiting for the water table.

### Gaps
- No tropical-peat runoff coefficient by land cover from a measured source other than Amal; Amal's value is not in the full text of the 2021 paper (see existing note).
- Share of annual rain leaving as overland flow only (not total runoff): not found in Kurnianto's extracted text; the supplementary figures (Fig. S9c, S9d) show surface versus subsurface runoff components but were not read.
- SIMGRO, PEATCLSM and DigiBog_Hydro on overland routing to ditches (DigiBog read here only for Putra's setup).

## 4. Canal water balance in the dry season: outlet and tidal control, evaporation, seepage, typical stage fall

### Takeaway
No published canal water balance with evaporation, seepage and outlet terms for a tropical peat canal was found. What exists: Kurnianto 2026 prescribes the tide at the outlet and no-flow at the upstream end; Putra 2022 sets ditch levels per season; RSPO and Hooijer (in existing notes) give plantation stage behaviour. Typical stage fall is documented only as ranges in the existing notes.

### Cited Findings
- Kurnianto 2026: downstream channel ends use measured tide (mean higher water 1.15 m above mean sea level; daily amplitude 1.12 m); direct tidal inundation confined to a narrow near-coastal fringe, and tidal influence inland "transmitted primarily through water levels in rivers and canals rather than through overland inundation across the peat surface" — [Kurnianto 2026, Sect. 2](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541143/1/N541143JA.pdf) (*full text*).
- Kurnianto 2026: peat underlain by impermeable marine clay, vertical percolation negligible, "all recharge and discharge processes are confined" laterally — [same](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541143/1/N541143JA.pdf) (*full text*). In managed land "water control infrastructure ... reduces hydraulic gradients between fields and canals, generating low runoff and a stable storage" in dry periods.
- West Kalimantan blocked canals had smaller water-table fluctuation over one tidal period than unblocked canals; a Dompas study reported canal blocking raised groundwater 10 to 21 cm with wetting beyond 75 m; a modelling study found 0.6 m blocks satisfactory at every canal distance with K = 50 m/d — [search summaries](https://ospro.usk.ac.id/article/wos/97302), [JOM Unri](https://jom.unri.ac.id/index.php/JOMFTEKNIK/article/view/26505) (*snippet*; papers not read).
- A water balance guideline note: in droughts a plantation drain can fall 0.5-1 cm/day (RSPO 2019, already in `canal_levels_evidence.md`; not re-read).

### Inferences
- Our unmeasured outlet constant (`--canal-tau`) has no published value to check against; the closest physical analogue is Kurnianto's tidal outlet, which would apply only to tidally connected KHG (Riau and Kalimantan coasts) and not to inland domes.
- Canal evaporation is small against ET from the peat surface and is not isolated in any source found; no number is offered.

### Gaps
- Canal evaporation and seepage terms, outlet rating curves, and measured canal stage series through a dry season for a drained peat canal (open data not found; Putra et al. 2021 ditch loggers are noted in the existing notes but not read here).
- Ritzema et al. 2014 and Sutikno et al. 2019 canal blocking papers were found by search but not read.

## Summary against the project's current approach

- **Sub-cell exchange:** no published peat model has an equivalent; Haitjema 2005 gives the analytic recipe for a resisted stream and Mehl and Hill 2010 warn that conductance is grid-dependent. LEAK_K = 12 is the no-bed-resistance limit; test it at 50 m and 100 m.
- **Canal stage fixed per class:** matches DigiBog (fixed Dirichlet, changed seasonally). Urzainki and MIKE Hydro treat stage as a state with a downstream boundary (no-flow or tide). The existing lumped store lies between them.
- **Ponded water removed:** same as DigiBog's 5 cm tank. Routing it to canals is a source-term change, not an SRO coefficient.
- **SRO coefficient to canals (C = 0.12):** contradicted as a clean idea by Kurnianto 2026, whose runoff is total (mostly subsurface in managed land); adding C × P to canals would double count where the groundwater model already drains the peat.
