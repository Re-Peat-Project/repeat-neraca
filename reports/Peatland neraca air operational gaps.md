# Closing the gap from peat deficit diagnostic to water-sharing service

repeat-neraca today delivers one validated quantity, a one-season KHG-level water deficit computed as a mass balance from observed water-table change. It is not yet a predictive water-balance model, and it contains no water-sharing logic. The largest missing piece is not a satellite product. The repo's own tests show that rain-minus-evaporation with passive canals cannot reproduce water tables that concessions hold at **35-55 cm** through the dry season, so the missing inputs are management facts (canal and weir levels, gate and pump states, tidal or river inflow) that no open satellite supplies. Spatial skill is about zero in every test run so far, forcing is a single 25 km ERA5 series per KHG, validation is one 2026 season on two KHGs, and there is no scheduler, forecast mode, uncertainty ensemble or allocation code. Satellite data can fill four of the gaps: finer and bias-checked rain and ET, SAR-based wetness as a relative spatial indicator, InSAR (including NISAR L-band) for subsidence and surface motion, and open canal and terrain layers. The notes find no published centimetre-level RMSE for satellite water-table retrieval over tropical peat. Satellites should therefore feed forcing and relative-wetness checks, not replace loggers. The recommended sequence is: (1) fix the validation and management-data foundation, (2) upgrade forcing, (3) build canal-storage coupling and an observed-level boundary condition, (4) define water sharing as target-level storage allocation, (5) add uncertainty and forecasting, and (6) automate.

## What the repo does today, and why it stops short

The repo holds two model families. The current "physical" family has a lumped KHG water-table ODE (`Sy(z) dz/dt = R - C(z)(z - z_c)`), a 2D Boussinesq solver on a 500 m grid, and a closed-form deficit, `deficit_mm = max(0, -sum(P-E) - Sy*dz_obs*10)` with Sy fixed at 0.15 (`src/repeat_neraca/lumped.py`, `spatial.py`, `deficit.py`). The bucket family was declared failed on spatial skill and is kept as a record (`docs/bucket_experiments_README.md`). The deficit results are the only output the repo recommends using: **290 mm (band 227-296), about 1,842 million m3, over Sugihan-Lumpur's 6,348 km2, and 7 mm (0-12) over Kampar-Gaung's 7,115 km2** (`docs/neraca-air.md`). It needs observed water-table change, so it cannot run ahead of the season.

The predictive components fail against stations. Tier 1 in Sugihan-Lumpur has simulated mean 65.7 cm against observed 43.0, bias +22.7 cm, RMSE 56.6 cm, but r = 0.96; the no-skill mean curve has RMSE 5.7 cm (`docs/reports/neraca_sugihan_lumpur_tier1.md`, bucket forcing test). Kampar-Gaung Tier 1 has bias -16.9 cm and r 0.68. Tier 2 across-station Spearman is -0.13 for mean level and 0.17 for peak depth, and `neraca_skill_ok = 0`. The bucket's out-of-fold RMSE was 16.5 cm against a 15.3 cm baseline, with spatial Spearman about -0.03. Adopting tropical peat tables improves some RMSEs, but the repo itself says this "is not independent evidence" because those sets were calibrated at another site. The structural diagnosis in `docs/neraca-air.md` is that the Sugihan-Lumpur June-October balance of P-E = -306 mm implies a 1.5-2 m fall at Sy 0.14-0.2, whereas stations fell 14 cm. About 285 mm (roughly 3 mm/day) of water enters from outside the rain balance, through tide, river inflow, pumping, gates or an ET overestimate. The repo says which of these is unresolved. This is the central methodological gap: the model has no supply term and no managed canal level.

The `config/parameters.yaml` registry lists pump, gate, relay, allocation-horizon, priority and subsidence parameters (`pump_hours`, `gate_band_*`, `alloc_horizon`, `subsidence_drained_mean` 2.2 cm/yr). A code search found no run code that uses them. The registry is a design specification, not an implementation. The Re-peat hub likewise lists the "water-sharing hydrology model (Tier 1/Tier 2)" as not yet coded, and its methodology plans compartment-scale canal-storage hydrology feeding a weekly rolling-horizon allocation LP, scenarios S0-S5, Monte Carlo and a dashboard (`re-peat/docs/Peatland Rewetting Water-Sharing Model - Methodology.md`, `docs/08-build-plan.md`). In the hub's own terms, "water sharing" is intra-KHG allocation of canal, gate and pump water, not basin-scale river allocation. The gap analysis below uses that meaning.

## Water sharing: no external allocation precedent, so demand must be defined

The notes found no Indonesian instrument that allocates volumes among users. Peat law uses function zoning and a single head criterion: peat is damaged if groundwater is 0.4 m or deeper below the surface at a compliance point, with at least 15% of main-crop plots designated as compliance points ([Mongabay legal notes](https://mongabay.co.id/2016/12/15/catatan-hukum-peraturan-gambut-lama-vs-baru/)). Proposals for 0.6 m from business and the Agriculture Ministry were rejected ([Mongabay changes](https://mongabay.co.id/2016/12/05/presiden-tandatangani-revisi-aturan-gambut-berikut-poin-poin-perubahan/amp/)). The closest formal analogue to operating rules is the tidal-swamp regime in Permen PUPR 11/2015, whose annex ties a cropping plan and water-management plan to operating procedures for gates ([PUPR 11/2015](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/152412/PermenPUPR11-2015.pdf)), with seasonal, weekly and daily plans in the lebak equivalent ([PUPR 16/2015](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/152425/PermenPUPR16-2015.pdf)). The notes did not read the full regulation texts, so article-level compliance rules (daily versus annual criterion, whether PP 57/2016 has since been amended) are unverified.

The inference that follows is mine and not sourced: water-sharing demand should be a target-level storage per land-use class (protected peat, acacia and other plantation, oil palm, smallholder, paludiculture), not a flow demand. The allocation problem is a competition between the 0.4 m regulatory ceiling, the 0.6 m fire level ([Taufik et al. via ASEAN Haze Portal](https://hazeportal.asean.org/?p=12604)) and a crop-specific flood ceiling (the core conflict visible in the 0.6 m debate). Proposed indicators, unsourced and without an Indonesian precedent, are share of days shallower than 0.4 and 0.6 m, storage deficit relative to target storage, fraction of demand met per user class, and equity as min/max or Gini of compliance across zones. The hub's methodology already specifies a weekly rolling-horizon min-cost-flow allocation with priority weights, a strategy tree, and six headline indicators. What is missing is code and, more fundamentally, inputs: the same documents say concession water-level targets, weir operating rules, pump inventories and canal levels have no open source. Governance is also undocumented, since no source describes who may operate shared blocks across concessions and villages.

Existing optimisation work addresses block siting, not sharing. Urzainki et al. couple a Boussinesq model with genetic algorithm and simulated annealing on a 931 km2 Sumatran peatland, using a single objective, water table at fixed cost ([BG 2020](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/17/4769/2020/)). Their 2023 successor adds a diffusive-wave canal module and Manning friction, validated only qualitatively against 141 dipwells ([BG 2023](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/20/2099/2023/)). The 2020 paper states it did not validate against field data. Re-peat's own benchmark could reproduce the stored canal-rise results exactly but the optimised-versus-random ratios only with transmissivity scaled by 100 (hub `docs/08-build-plan.md`). A neraca that publishes quantitative dipwell skill scores would itself be a contribution, and the repo should treat block effects as small in absolute terms (80 blocks moved the annual mean from -21.45 to -20.08 cm in the Urzainki 2020 example per the hub notes).

## Satellite water-table retrieval has no cm-level skill to rely on, which shapes validation and uncertainty

The notes are candid about evidence limits. Satellite water-table retrieval has no published centimetre-level RMSE over Indonesian peat in anything the researchers surfaced. The reported numbers are correlations: Sentinel-1 VV r = -0.648 at one Riau site (implied R2 about 0.42, my arithmetic), OPTRAM best-pixel R 0.7-1 under low NDVI and near zero under forest, and SMOS assimilation that raised mean water-level correlation from 0.46 to 0.63 at four Congo sites ([EGU22-7430](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU22/EGU22-7430.html), conference abstract). The only cm-level figure for canal water depth comes from airborne LiDAR, not a satellite: 86% within 0.25 m, R2 = 0.74 against 145 field measurements ([Vernimmen et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/w12051486)). Most reported skills are site-specific, calibrated in-sample, and untested for cross-site transfer. Several citations in the notes are abstract-level or search summaries only, including Widodo et al. 2019 (the 88% versus 5% fire-association figures need checking), the 2025 Remote Sensing of Environment InSAR paper (authors and DOI not captured) and the EGU25 InSAR abstract.

The peat hydrology notes carry similar limits. They found no verified Sy-versus-WTD curve, no Kc values for oil palm, Acacia, forest or scrub on peat, and no tropical-peat skill numbers for DRAINMOD, MODFLOW, SWAT, HYDRUS or ParFlow. The verified ones are: statistical water-table model RMSE 16.2 cm, R2 81% ([Febrianti 2018](https://journal.ipb.ac.id/index.php/jtanah/article/view/29278)); DRAINMOD RMSE 2.75 cm on non-peat tidal mineral soil ([Unsri](https://repository.unsri.ac.id/24892/)); PEATCLSM-Trop reporting only "improved bias and correlation" with strong regional differences attributed to forcing accuracy and unrepresented hydrologic response ([White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/214913)). Saturated K spans 0.001 to 13.9 m/day over 19 West Kalimantan sites, so a single value is not defensible ([USGS summary](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/influence-land-cover-changes-variability-saturated-hydraulic-conductivity-tropical)). The practical consequence is that a roughly 16 cm RMSE is the only verified benchmark to beat, and that any satellite-calibrated skill claim needs logger-based, held-out validation, with best-pixel correlations treated as upper bounds. No GLUE, DREAM or ensemble Kalman applications to tropical peat were found. Published station counts used in validation are small (8 stations in Taufik et al. 2022, 12 in a SMAP bias correction), and no formal minimum sample size or community protocol exists.

Validation data are available but fragmented. The notes list the 8-station Batanghari and Kubu Raya daily dataset ([Taufik et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2022.107903)), Sebangau loggers ([Leeds DOI 10.5518/960](https://doi.org/10.5518/960)), the 96-site collection used by [Apers et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9285420/), and the BRGM SIPALAGA network, with hourly availability 76-93% and 17% of records missing in one 2019-2021 study ([record](https://www.tropicalpeatlands.org/knowledge/publication/11463-17530)). Open-licence status was not confirmed for any of these, no open canal level series was found, and Re-peat's holding of SiPALAGA is only four weeks from 2019.

## Sequenced roadmap

The sequence follows dependency: each stage unlocks the next, and the first two make every later number defensible.

**Stage 1: foundation (weeks 1-6).** Add NSE and KGE to `validate.py`, set skill against both persistence and the station-mean baseline, and make "beat the no-skill baseline" a hard acceptance gate. Tests for `validate.py`, the loader on real data and `run_neraca.py` end to end are absent and should be added. Fix the contract mismatches: read stations through `repeat_common.tma`, standardise `khg_id`, point the ranking at the physical `neraca_unit.csv`, wire the registry's tropical peat sets and `k_forest/k_drained`, and add CI. Request the multi-season record from SiTMAT or BRGM and any open sets above; baseline years 2019-2021 cannot be validated until some water-table record exists for them. Confirm the data-licence terms in the notes' gaps (SIPALAGA, BMKG, BRGM peat layers).

**Stage 2: forcing (weeks 4-10).** Build a spatial P and ET forcing layer, not a single KHG series. Candidate rain: CHIRPS v3 (via Earth Engine, avoiding the burst-read 403) and IMERG V07 Late, rescaled to gauges where available; note SiTMAT's `rain_fall_mm` column as an untested in-situ rain source. Candidate ET: GLEAM4 and PML_V2 as cross-checks against ERA5 and WaPOR, with land-cover-dependent ET. Report all products as a multi-forcing band (the deficit band already does this for two). Evaluate each against SiTMAT rain and logger responses in the one season available, and treat results as one-season evidence.

**Stage 3: canal storage and a supply boundary (weeks 8-20).** This is the step the repo's own diagnosis names as decisive. Implement canal cells as storage with a stage state (the `testbed/canals.py` topology is a starting point and `repeat-pump` already holds a canal graph, so reuse rather than duplicate the solver, which currently exists in two repos). Replace "surface minus 1.2 m" with an observed or logger-inferred canal stage per concession and a supply term at the KHG edge for tide and river. Without canal levels the model reproduces the concession's management rather than predicting it. Test this stage on the existing 2026 data first, then on a held-out concession. Compare against a flopy MODFLOW 6 twin and the blopti core as a numerical cross-check; the tooling notes suggest this ladder (blopti, MODFLOW 6, PEATCLSM outputs, a gradient-boosting baseline), and the Siak synthetic case works as a regression test.

**Stage 4: satellite spatial layers as relative checks (weeks 12-26, parallel).** Use Sentinel-1 only as within-concession relative wetness with logger calibration, given the repo's negative across-concession result, and add NISAR L-band tests for subsidence once the provisional record is confirmed. Only for open or degraded peat, consider OPTRAM. Do not use any of these as an absolute water-table input until held-out logger RMSE in cm exists; the notes found none. Terrain remains the binding constraint: either obtain LiDAR for a pilot KHG (Sugihan has LiDAR contours per the hub notes) or re-reference the DTM to logger elevations.

**Stage 5: water-sharing logic (weeks 20-36).** Define demand as target-level storage per land-use class, choose target levels (0.4 m regulatory, 0.6 m fire, crop flood ceiling), then implement allocation from internal surplus, gravity transfer and pumping, first greedy and then LP with rolling horizon, per the hub methodology. Report per-class compliance, fraction of target storage met and a spread-based equity metric. Obtain pump inventories and gate and weir operating rules; per the hub notes these are not open. Decide the primary user (KLH/BPLH versus BPGMN), which is an open question in the hub's methodology.

**Stage 6: uncertainty and forecasting (weeks 28-44).** Run Monte Carlo over the registry's sampled ranges, plus forcing-product ensemble and terrain error, and cache offline per KHG. Add forecast forcing: ECMWF SEAS5 had skill at 3-5 month leads for fire probability in southern regions ([NHESS preprint](https://nhess.copernicus.org/preprints/nhess-2021-245/nhess-2021-245.pdf)), the BMKG 3-day forecast and Fire Danger Rating layers could serve short leads, but SEAS5 open-data licensing and the BMKG access route (API through PTSP, scraping not permitted, PNBP fees possible; [BMKG terms](https://www.bmkg.go.id/ketentuan-penggunaan)) are unverified. Publish a skill score per KHG and say plainly where there is no skill, following the GloFAS verification pattern ([HESS 2023](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/27/1/2023/)).

**Stage 7: operations (weeks 36+).** Automate with config-driven runs, versioned outputs with registry snapshot, and a scheduler (Prefect or Airflow in production, a GitHub Actions cron for demos; this recommendation is engineering judgement, not sourced, and the notes did not compare them). Store forcing in Zarr and publish COG/STAC with CF attributes per the standards inference. Stand up a data contract for the dashboard, which the hub lacks.

## Conclusion

The sharpest finding is that the "operational water-sharing analysis" is limited more by management data than by satellite coverage. The repo's own evidence points to a canal level held by weirs and an unexplained external inflow of roughly 3 mm/day. The most useful satellite contribution is improved forcing and a relative-wetness cross-check, not a replacement for loggers, because no published cm-level satellite water-table skill was found. Rebuilding the model around observed canal stage, with a validation protocol that gates every product on beating a no-skill baseline, matters more than adding another dataset.

Three further implications follow. First, the deficit diagnostic is already the output that pump sizing consumes, so improving it (multi-season, finer forcing, depth-dependent Sy with a band) delivers value before the full model exists. Second, a quantitative dipwell skill score on an independent year and KHG would itself be novel against the Urzainki-line literature, which never validated quantitatively. Third, several inputs the plan depends on (concession targets, operating rules, pump inventories, governance) cannot be obtained from open data, so the data-sharing agreements with KLH/BPLH, BRGM and concession operators are on the critical path as much as any code.
