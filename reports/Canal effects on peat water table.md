# Open canals drain peat, but dry ones do not

Across all sources read, no study shows a shallower dry-season water table beside an open, unblocked, water-carrying canal than far from it. Where the near-canal water table is deeper, the clearest numbers are from Ritzema et al. 2014 (Kalampangan Canal): in the dry season 0.88 m deep at 10 m, 0.54 m at 100 m and 0.33 m at 1000 m ([Ritzema et al. 2014](https://luk.tsipil.ugm.ac.id/rawa/PLG/1-s2.0-S0341816213002531-main.pdf), full text). Shallower near-canal readings appear in three situations: blocked canals, canals that are dry or held low (Putra et al. 2021, Sebangau), and ground that has subsided beside the canal so that depth below the local surface is small. This matches the Mendahara-Batanghari puzzle, where four of five stations within 60 m of a canal read 13 to 35 cm; the published data cannot separate the three explanations, and the dry-canal and subsided-margin explanations are the two with measured numbers. The published peat models do not resolve the canal in the way the RE-PEAT 100 m model must: Urzainki 2023 and Kurnianto 2026 solve canal stage as a state in a 1D network, Putra 2022 fixes ditch levels per season, and none has a sub-cell correction for a canal narrower than the cell. No open satellite product gives a validated 100 m water-table map on Indonesian peat. The useful ones are Dadap 2021 as an audit layer for canal completeness, InSAR subsidence by distance to canal as a relative pattern check, and ICESat-2 canal levels as priors. A MODFLOW 6 test is worth a short, gated trial for its scenario and package features, but it will not fix the canal boundary, which the notes identify as the input problem. One earlier project statement needs correcting: the "under 5 cm by about 300 m" result of Kurnianto 2026 holds only with dams; without dams the model gives 19 cm at 100 m and an effect that never reaches zero, even at 1 km.

Source status is stated per claim: full text, abstract or snippet (a search summary, not verified against the paper). Several notes were limited by paywalls and blocked publisher pages; those gaps are listed where they matter.

## Field evidence: drawdown depends on whether the canal holds water

The only repeated distance profile from an unblocked canal is Ritzema et al. 2014 (Central Kalimantan, 11 wells either side of the canal up to 500 m, one line extended to 3 km, monitored 2004 to 2009). The table gives water table depth below the surface, averaged over four transects ([Ritzema et al. 2014](https://luk.tsipil.ugm.ac.id/rawa/PLG/1-s2.0-S0341816213002531-main.pdf), full text).

| Distance from canal | 10 m | 100 m | 400 m | 1000 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dry season (Jul-Oct), m | 0.88 | 0.54 | 0.35 | 0.33 |
| Rainy season (Nov-Apr), m | 0.50 | 0.13 | 0.05 | 0.06 |

The profile has a steep part within about 100 m (0.21 m deeper than at 1000 m in the dry season, arithmetic from the table) and a tail within about 0.02 m of the 1000 m value by 400 m. The same paper shows the canal cutting dome flow: the downstream side is deeper than the dome side (dry season 0.84 vs 0.52 m in degraded peat). Dams on that canal raised the water table to 0.26 to 0.35 m below ground in the first months, against 1.02 to 1.22 m before, but it fell again to well below 0.40 m the following dry season, which the authors attribute to lateral seepage through permeable peat and under the dams.

Where a canal is blocked, the canal level drives the peat water table over 100 to 200 m. At Sungai Tohor, Pulau Tebing Tinggi (the test area, full text), raising a 4 m wide, 3 m deep canal by 0.69 m raised the water table 0.63, 0.29, 0.13 and 0.04 m at 20, 70, 120 and 170 m, with no response at 220 m; transmitted fractions of 0.91, 0.42, 0.19 and 0.06 are my arithmetic ([Sutikno et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1051/matecconf/201927606003)). The decay scale of roughly 60 to 80 m is an estimate in the notes from those four points, not a published value, and the authors note the reach was capped by topography. At Dompas, Bengkalis, blocking raised the water table 10 to 21 cm with a rise flat across 0.5 to 75.5 m, so the decay length was not observed ([Alfath et al. 2020](https://jom.unri.ac.id/index.php/JOMFTEKNIK/article/download/26505/25624), full text, Indonesian). A kilometre-scale gradient exists in a retired, blocked Acacia plantation in South Sumatra: 7.5-year mean depth of 0.63 m in the first 700 m from the main road canal, falling to 0.25 m beyond 3.5 km ([Hooijer et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-60462-3), full text). That resolution of 700 m says nothing about the first 50 m.

The case closest to Mendahara in structure is Putra, Holden and Baird 2021 at Tumbang Nusa, Sebangau (small unblocked ditches in degraded, burned peat). In the wet period the water table was 37.2 cm deep in wells near ditches (mean ditch distance 21 and 29 m) and 20.5 cm in the well 173 m away, but in the dry period the well-minus-ditch difference was under 10 cm everywhere because the ditches were dry and water did not flow to them ([Putra et al. 2021](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/179900/1/hyp.14174.pdf), full text). Dry-season drawdown there was driven by evapotranspiration (9.3 to 12.7 mm/d). Hooijer et al. 2024 add a second quantified case: fully blocked canals without bypasses silted with vegetation, with open water falling to 26% on average (range 7 to 45%) ([Hooijer et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-60462-3), full text). No survey gives the share of canals holding water in the dry season, or canal level against peat water table at network scale outside Hooijer's plantations.

In managed plantations the canal sets the water table. Hooijer 2024 estimated groundwater depth as canal water depth plus 0.05 m, explained by field drains under 200 m apart and a peat conductivity of 119 m/d ([Hooijer et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-60462-3), full text), and Vernimmen and Hooijer 2026 state canal levels are typically less than 0.1 m below peat levels ([Vernimmen and Hooijer 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fenvs.2026.1869652), full text). Measured canal depth is ordered by elevation and crop, not canal class: about -0.2 m at 1 to 3 m elevation, -0.6 to -0.85 m for oil palm and Acacia at 5 to 9 m. No source measures canal level by canal class. Published managed-canal values (about -0.2 to -0.85 m) bracket the 0.6 m that fitted community stations, while the 1.2 m used in the model comes from Urzainki 2020, unvalidated, and matches unmanaged dry-season canals ([Vernimmen et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/w12051486), full text).

Influence distance depends on the question. A change in canal level reaches 100 to 200 m (Sutikno, Dompas). The drainage drawdown of an open dome reaches several hundred metres at the 2 cm level (Ritzema) and kilometres at the few-centimetre level: Ismail et al. 2018 report drawdown reaching a minimum 3 km from the ditch ([EGU abstract](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2018/EGU2018-17323.pdf), snippet). "Only tens to a few hundred metres" therefore holds for level changes at the canal, not for the whole drainage effect. A related unverified point: on Pulau Padang, Ismail et al. 2021 report large-scale plantation drainage with water table about -1.8 m and recession up to 3.5 cm/day ([Ismail et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.2166/nh.2021.062), snippet; full text not reached).

## How published peat models represent the canal boundary

Three reference models were read in full. Urzainki 2023 solves canal stage with a diffusive wave on a 50 m canal grid and passes exchange through the Darcy flow of the adjacent peat cell; canal-to-canal faces carry zero transmissivity, and there is no separate seepage coefficient ([Urzainki 2023](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/20/2099/2023/), full text, Table 1 not read). The authors state the coupling does not strictly conserve mass and that modelled blocks are watertight although real blocks are porous ([author response](https://bg.copernicus.org/preprints/bg-2022-218/bg-2022-218-ATC1.pdf), full text). Kurnianto 2026 couples MIKE SHE to 1D MIKE Hydro through river links, with a calibrated leakage coefficient of 1.0e-4 (range 5e-5 to 2e-4, unit printed as "(-)"), 123 broad-crested weirs with crests 0.6 m below ground, no-flow at upstream ends and measured tide downstream ([Kurnianto 2026](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541143/1/N541143JA.pdf), full text). Putra, Baird and Holden 2022 (DigiBog_Hydro, 1 m cells) set ditch water level as a Dirichlet value, constant within each of four roughly 100-day periods, with dams acting by changing the set level; dams alone affected only 26 m (moderately degraded) and 12 m (severely degraded) laterally in the dry season ([Putra et al. 2022](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/188257/), full text; distances from a snippet).

None of these corrects for a canal narrower than the cell. The RE-PEAT construction (leak factor of 12 from the strip solution, scaled by cell size) is therefore different from all three; only the MIKE leakage coefficient and MODFLOW-type drain conductance are of the same head-dependent kind, and the former is calibrated, not derived. The strip solution has no bed resistance and no radial convergence, which in Haitjema's terms is the case where leakage length is small ([Haitjema 2005](https://haitjema.com/documents/Dealingwithresistancetoflowintosurfacewaters.pdf), full text). That is defensible for open canals in permeable peat and will overstate exchange for silted or blocked beds. Mehl and Hill 2010 found stream leakage differed by up to 122% as grids were refined and that conductances do not transfer between grid sizes ([Mehl and Hill 2010](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70037505), abstract). The notes therefore propose a test, not a published correction: run the same canal at 100 m and 50 m and check whether leakage per canal length changes by tens of percent. The classical alternative is Hooghoudt's equivalent depth, which absorbs radial convergence using a ditch radius of wetted perimeter over pi ([Drainage equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_equation), page read); no tropical peat application with measured ditch levels and wells, and no published value for 100 m cells, was found, and the entrance-resistance formula was not retrieved.

Two choices follow from the field evidence and the models. First, Putra's fixed-per-period ditch level is the closest published analogue to a fixed level per class, so a seasonal level is a cheap alternative to the lumped canal store; a floor of "canal level not below the water table" would reproduce the dry-ditch case, whereas a stage fixed at 0.8 and 0.3 m fails exactly where canals are dry, vegetation-choked or perched (inference, from Putra 2021 and Hooijer 2024). Second, the runoff coefficient: Kurnianto's modelled runoff is total drainage (surface plus subsurface), 6% of rain in the dry year and 35% in the wet year, and in managed land it is predominantly subsurface. Adding a surface runoff coefficient of 0.12 to canals on top of Boussinesq flow would double count; routing water removed as ponding to the nearest canal cell is the faithful change, and it matters only in the wet season ([Kurnianto 2026](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541143/1/N541143JA.pdf), full text; routing recommendation is inference).

## Open satellite data: audit canals and check patterns, not validate levels

No published r or R2 between Sentinel-1 backscatter and water table on Indonesian peat was found. The tropical evidence is InSAR. Mleczko et al. 2025 (Central Kalimantan, Sentinel-1 SBAS 2017 to 2022) obtained displacement over only 34.4 to 59.8% of the peat area, with correlations to groundwater level more varied in the dry season ([NORA record](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/540352/), abstract). Izumi et al. 2025 report peat surface oscillation linearly related to water table with r 0.23 to 0.8, representing 2.8 to 8.3% of water table variation ([Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/875115b95e7f5692b18e1f74f1d81130/?utm_source=claude_desktop), abstract). Setyaningrum et al. 2026 find subsidence largest around drainage canals, with r = -0.44 against groundwater level ([Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/785b7c97329d57e2bab556c896fe6a6a/?utm_source=claude_desktop), abstract), and Tarigan et al. 2023 find more subsidence in used than blocked zones in the Merang-Ngirawan KHG ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2023.1088923/full), page read). The only quantitative backscatter skill is temperate: in a German drained fen the correlation drops once the water table is deeper than about 0.6 m ([Asmuss et al. 2019](https://consensus.app/papers/details/c3205572515b5310af157a610585351e/?utm_source=claude_desktop), abstract). L-band HH correlated 0.63 to 0.84 with relative level under intact forest on one Central Kalimantan dome ([ESA TPV final report](https://eo4society.esa.int/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/ESA-TPV-Final-report.pdf), full text). The notes conclude Sentinel-1 gives relative wetness and timing, not level, and that a model-versus-InSAR comparison by distance-to-canal band is the one direct pattern check, since the model predicts a distance pattern. The full texts of Mleczko, Izumi and Setyaningrum were not read, so station counts and land covers behind those figures are unknown.

For the incomplete, mis-classed canal layer, the cheapest audit is Dadap et al. 2021, an open 5 m canal raster from July to September 2017 Planet imagery (CC BY 3.0); a note in a search result says it treats roads as canals ([AGU Advances](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020AV000321), abstract; road caveat snippet). It will miss canals dug after 2017, and its validation numbers were not retrievable. A Sentinel-1 canal-gap detector detected 85.5% of reference canal length but below 50% for 5 to 10 m widths and for canals more than 80 degrees from the azimuth, applies to forest canals only, and no open code was found ([ESA TPV final report](https://eo4society.esa.int/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/ESA-TPV-Final-report.pdf), full text). No study tests JRC Global Surface Water or Sentinel-2 NDWI for water in peat canals; canals of a few metres lie below 10 to 30 m pixels, so an optical holds-water test is plausible only for wide primary canals and is untested.

For canal level, ICESat-2 plus a DTM is the only satellite route that has produced canal water depth at scale on Indonesian peat: 15,575 crossings from 2018 to 2025, of which 64% were on peat ([Vernimmen and Hooijer 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fenvs.2026.1869652), page read to character 100,000 of 124,720). It has no same-date field validation, assumes a 0.75 m geoid offset, excludes near north-south canals, and covers only wide open canals; Jambi has no statistics. The workspace holds ATL08 only; the method needs ATL03 photons. The airborne-LiDAR predecessor reached R2 0.74 and 86% within 0.25 m on 145 sites, but only for canals over 3 m wide with a 1 m DTM and 100 m cells ([Vernimmen et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/w12051486), full text). OPTRAM correlated R 0.7 to 1 with water table on open or low-NDVI tropical peat with a per-station best-pixel pick, but only -0.04 to 0.24 under high NDVI ([Koupaei-Abyazani et al. 2024](https://pubs.ssec.wisc.edu/pub/view/15698), abstract). SMAP L4 v8 has a peat water-table band at 11 km pixel, giving one to ten pixels per KHG, with tropical parameter status unconfirmed ([Earth Engine catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/NASA_SMAP_SPL4SMGP_008), page read). GRACE is too coarse. For forcing, rainfall-product comparisons in Indonesia were found only as unverified snippets and rank products inconsistently, so swapping CHIRPS is unlikely to help unless local station rain shows a bias. DEM error under canopy (metres) exceeds the head differences the model resolves, so canal-relative levels should come from local LiDAR where it exists.

## A MODFLOW 6 test is worth a short, gated trial, not a KHG-scale rebuild

Published MODFLOW use on tropical peat is thin: two FREEWAT/MODFLOW studies on domains around 300 x 1000 m with constant K per layer and a canal boundary head, calibrated only near one canal ([Chalid et al.](https://www.academia.edu/50651044/The_Modelling_of_Groundwater_Table_Management_for_Canal_Blocking_Scenarios_In_Sub_Peatland_Hydrological_Unit), partial page text). No MODFLOW 6 or NWT tropical peat paper was found, so a trial would have no published skill to benchmark against. The Groundwater Project chapter on peat names the exponential decrease of K with depth as the main discretisation challenge ([GW Project 7.1](https://books.gw-project.org/groundwater-in-peat-and-peatlands/chapter/7-1-numerical-flow-and-transport-models/), full text).

The documented technical facts are favourable on the parts that are mechanical. MODFLOW 6 gives each cell one K and one Sy, so the exponential T(zeta) must be built from layers; Newton keeps cells from drying, and `ALTERNATIVE_CELL_AVERAGING`, `HIGHEST_CELL_SATURATION`, `TVK6`, the drain package (conductance, depth-scaled discharge), `WEL`, `EVT` and the mover are all documented ([NPF](https://modflow6.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_mf6io/gwf-npf.html), [DRN](https://modflow6.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_mf6io/gwf-drn.html), [NAM](https://modflow6.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_mf6io/gwf-nam.html); doc pages). Lu et al. 2024 warn that MODFLOW 6 inherits the weaknesses of NWT, with first-order upstream weighting and a need for smoothing, so exact agreement with a Picard code is not expected near dry cells ([Lu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022WR034334), page via extractor). Flopy installs the binary with `get-modflow`, and `modflowapi` (CC0) supports Python-driven per-step feedback, shown in published agricultural and treatment packages ([FloPy docs](https://flopy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/md/get_modflow.html); [modflowapi](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflowapi)). The untested risks are convergence of thin top layers with Newton, run time and memory at about 500,000 cells and 330 daily periods, and the managed-cell top-up with a supply cap, which needs an API loop or a fixed-head shortcut that loses the cap. Mapping of canals, blocks, pumps and ponding removal to CHD, DRN, WEL and a surface drain is inference; SFR, LAK, MVR, CHD, RIV and RCH pages were not fetched, and the MODFLOW 6 licence was not checked before bundling a binary in the plugin.

My judgement from these notes: the earlier conclusion stands that the equation is not the fault, the inputs are. A MODFLOW 6 run would reuse the same canal boundary, so on its own it cannot explain why Mendahara stations disagree with the model. Its value is a cross-check of the solver, access to SFR/LAK canal networks later, and a maintained code for the QGIS plugin. That justifies the cheap early steps of the proposed trial (days 1 to 5: environment, 1D strip against the Dupuit solution, layered T and Sy strip, small 2D block with canal, block and pump) with gates set before running, and a stop if thin layers fail to converge. The KHG-scale run should wait until the canal boundary changes below are in.

## Where these notes correct or conflict with earlier project notes

The earlier note `canal_levels_evidence.md` quotes Kurnianto 2026 as finding the drainage effect under 5 cm by about 300 m. The full text shows that holds only with dams (9 cm at 100 m, below 5 cm at about 300 m); without dams the effect is 19 cm at 100 m and remains beyond 300 m, and "these effects never approached zero, even at 1 km". The 5 cm level also equals the model RMSE ([Kurnianto 2026](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541143/)). The same note's contrast with Ismail 2018 (3 km, open degraded peat) therefore reflects dams versus no dams as much as peat condition. A second correction: the Chalid et al. FREEWAT paper is a different paper from Suryadi et al. 2021, and their details must not be merged. A third: the earlier lead that Urzainki 2023's peat module contains a mathematical error is neither confirmed nor refuted by the author response read; the reviewer comments were not read. Two items remain unresolved: Kurnianto 2019 reports forest peat K of 0.63 m/d and oil palm 0.20 m/d, against the 8.6 to 173 m/d range used so far ([IPC 2016 abstract](https://peatlands.org/assets/uploads/2019/06/ipc16p165-168kurniantopeterson.etal_.pdf), snippet), with method and depth differences unexplained; and independent measurements give 0.6 to 0.85 m at higher plantation elevations while the repo's analysis found concessions holding 35 to 55 cm, where the notes list compliance-point siting, years covered and elevation as untested reasons. Putra 2021's dry-season behaviour was a snippet in the exchange note and is upgraded here to full text.

## Conclusion

The notes shift the question from "how strong is the canal effect" to "is there water in the canal". Open, water-carrying canals drain peat in every transect read, but the same peat shows no gradient when the ditch is dry, and blocked canals act as sources. For Mendahara, a fixed canal depth is the assumption most likely to disagree with stations, and the fix is a seasonal or floored canal level, which the evidence supports before any change of solver or data source.

Two limits remain. No source measures canal level by class, the share of dry canals, or station distance to canal in the SIPALAGA, SATREPS or SiTMAT networks (searches found none), so the project's own station-by-distance analysis would be new. And every satellite option checks pattern or supplies priors; none validates a 100 m water table, so the roughly 20 stations stay the main test, with the gates written before the runs as the project requires.
