## 3. What this means for our results

1. **Every model that works in drained tropical peat treats drainage as an explicit input**: a canal water level (SWAP), a prescribed lateral boundary water table (`ecosys`), or ditch depth, interval and conductivity (PEATCLSM-Trop). None predicts the water table from rain minus evaporation alone, which is what our bucket did. Our failure, and the 64% share of variance explained by concession in Kampar-Gaung, is what these papers would predict.
2. **Our fitted specific yield of 3.0** (against 0.1-0.3 in the literature) shows the bucket absorbing the missing drainage term.
3. **Two usable ideas.** (a) Following Taufik et al., a canal water level per concession and season could be inferred from the station records themselves and used as the boundary condition. **Tested** (`python -m repeat_neraca.bucket.canal_level`, README "Fifth test"): it works for concessions that have stations (RMSE 12.4 against 14.6 cm in Kampar-Gaung) but not better than copying a neighbour station (10.9 cm), and it has no skill for a concession without stations. (b) Mezbahuddin et al. validate against SMAP soil moisture, a spatial check that needs no station network; it would test the rain-minus-evaporation balance per sub-KHG at about 9 km.
4. **What is still unknown:** how well these models do against station water tables in a managed concession; the papers report soil moisture (Mezbahuddin) or site water levels (Apers) with correlations of 0.5-0.7, not the spatial ranking we need.
